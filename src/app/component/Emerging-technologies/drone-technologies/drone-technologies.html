FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Drone Technology FAQs

What is drone technology?
Drone technology involves the design and operation of unmanned aerial systems using motors, propellers, flight controllers, sensors, batteries, communication systems and software.
What is a UAV?
UAV stands for Unmanned Aerial Vehicle. It refers to an aircraft that operates without a person physically onboard.
How does a drone fly?
A drone uses motors and propellers to generate and control the forces required for flight. Motor-speed changes control movements such as throttle, roll, pitch and yaw.
What sensors are used in drones?
Depending on the drone, sensors can include accelerometers, gyroscopes, barometers, compasses, positioning systems and obstacle-detection sensors.
Is drone technology suitable for school students?
Yes. Age-appropriate drone programs can introduce students to flight, physics, electronics, sensors, programming, robotics and STEM concepts.
Do I need programming knowledge?
Not necessarily. Beginners can start with drone components, flight and control concepts before progressing into programming and automation.
Can drone technology be combined with AI?
Yes. AI and computer vision can be explored in areas such as image analysis, object detection, monitoring and other aerial-data applications.
What is the connection between drones and robotics?
Drones can be considered aerial robotic systems because they combine sensors, control systems, software and physical movement to perform tasks.
What can students learn through a drone workshop?
Students can explore drone components, flight principles, sensors, control systems, navigation, aerial imaging, programming concepts and emerging applications.
Can colleges conduct drone technology workshops?
Yes. Drone workshops can be structured for colleges and universities based on academic background, technical level and program objectives.
Where is drone technology used?
Drone technology is being used or explored in aerial imaging, surveying, agriculture, infrastructure inspection, construction, environmental monitoring, research and education.
