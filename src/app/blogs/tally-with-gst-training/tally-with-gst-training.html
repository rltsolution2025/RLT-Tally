GETTING STARTED

Introduction to TallyPrime Accounting Entries

Accounting entries are records of financial transactions made by a business. They help track money received, payments made, sales, purchases, expenses and other business activities.

TallyPrime is accounting software that helps users record transactions through vouchers and maintain business accounts. Understanding the accounting principles behind each entry makes it easier to select the appropriate voucher and ledger.

What should you understand first?

Identify the transaction, determine the accounts affected, understand debit and credit, select the suitable voucher, and verify the entry before saving it.

The examples in this guide are simplified learning examples. Actual accounting treatment can depend on the transaction, applicable accounting standards and business circumstances.

TALLYPRIME FUNDAMENTALS

Common Voucher Types in TallyPrime

Vouchers are used to record different kinds of business transactions. Choosing the correct voucher helps keep records organised and supports accurate reporting.

Payment

Record money paid to suppliers or for business expenses.

Receipt

Record money received from customers or other sources.

Sales

Record sales of goods or services.

Purchase

Record purchases of goods or services.

Contra

Record eligible cash and bank transfers.

Journal

Record eligible adjustment and journal transactions.

Credit Note

Record applicable sales returns and credit adjustments.

Debit Note

Record applicable purchase returns and debit adjustments.

Note: Voucher availability and the appropriate voucher type depend on the transaction and your TallyPrime configuration.

PRACTICAL EXAMPLES

Basic Accounting Entries with Examples

The following examples explain common transactions using simplified debit and credit entries. Use the examples to understand the accounting logic before entering transactions into TallyPrime.

The examples assume straightforward transactions without additional tax, discount, withholding or other adjustments unless specified.
01
Capital

Business Started with Cash

TRANSACTION

The owner introduces ₹50,000 as cash capital into the business.

Accounting Entry
Cash A/c Dr. ₹50,000
To Capital A/c ₹50,000
POSSIBLE VOUCHER Receipt

Learning point: Cash increases, and the owner's capital in the business increases.

02
Purchase

Goods Purchased for Cash

TRANSACTION

Goods are purchased for ₹10,000 and paid for immediately in cash.

Accounting Entry
Purchases A/c Dr. ₹10,000
To Cash A/c ₹10,000
POSSIBLE VOUCHER Purchase

Learning point: Record the purchase using the appropriate purchase ledger and cash ledger.

03
Sales

Goods Sold for Cash

TRANSACTION

Goods are sold for ₹15,000, and payment is received in cash.

Accounting Entry
Cash A/c Dr. ₹15,000
To Sales A/c ₹15,000
POSSIBLE VOUCHER Sales

Learning point: Cash received is debited, while sales income is credited.

04
Expense

Office Rent Paid

TRANSACTION

Office rent of ₹8,000 is paid in cash.

Accounting Entry
Rent A/c Dr. ₹8,000
To Cash A/c ₹8,000
POSSIBLE VOUCHER Payment

Learning point: Record the expense in the relevant expense ledger and credit the payment account.

05
Banking

Cash Deposited into Bank

TRANSACTION

Cash of ₹20,000 is deposited into the business bank account.

Accounting Entry
Bank A/c Dr. ₹20,000
To Cash A/c ₹20,000
POSSIBLE VOUCHER Contra

Learning point: A cash-to-bank transfer is generally recorded using a Contra voucher.

06
Credit Purchase

Goods Purchased on Credit

TRANSACTION

Goods worth ₹25,000 are purchased from a supplier on credit.

Accounting Entry
Purchases A/c Dr. ₹25,000
To Supplier A/c ₹25,000
POSSIBLE VOUCHER Purchase

Learning point: The supplier's outstanding balance is recorded through the appropriate party ledger.

Important about accounting entries

These examples are simplified and exclude GST. Actual entries may require tax ledgers, inventory details, party balances or other adjustments depending on the transaction and your accounting setup.

BUSINESS TRANSACTIONS

Sales and Purchase Entries in TallyPrime

Sales and purchase transactions are important parts of everyday accounting. In TallyPrime, the entry generally includes the party ledger, sales or purchase ledger, item details when applicable, and the payment or credit terms.

01

Create or verify the ledgers

Check the customer or supplier ledger and the relevant sales or purchase ledger.

02

Select the correct voucher

Use the appropriate Sales or Purchase voucher for the transaction.

03

Enter the transaction details

Record the date, party, item or service, quantity, rate and applicable tax details.

04

Review and save

Check the totals, tax treatment and ledger selection before saving the voucher.

Details to Check Before Saving

Transaction date Customer or supplier Sales or purchase ledger Item, quantity and rate Applicable GST details Invoice total and payment terms
GST ACCOUNTING

Understanding GST Entries in TallyPrime

When a transaction attracts GST, the accounting entry may include the relevant tax ledger in addition to the sales or purchase and party ledgers. The exact treatment depends on the transaction, place of supply, applicable tax rules and the company's GST configuration.

ACCURACY CHECK

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Beginners can improve accuracy by reviewing entries carefully and understanding why each ledger is used.

Selecting the wrong ledger

Verify whether the transaction involves a customer, supplier, expense, income, asset or liability ledger.

Choosing the wrong voucher

Understand the purpose of each voucher and select one that suits the transaction.

Entering an incorrect date

Check the invoice date, accounting period and applicable reporting period.

Incorrect tax configuration

Verify the transaction's GST treatment and tax details instead of assuming every transaction uses the same rate.

Not checking reports

Review ledger balances and relevant reports to help identify incorrect or incomplete entries.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an accounting entry in TallyPrime?

An accounting entry records a financial transaction using the relevant ledgers and voucher in TallyPrime. The entry reflects the accounting effect of the transaction.

Which voucher should beginners learn first?

Beginners can start by understanding Payment, Receipt, Contra, Journal, Sales and Purchase vouchers. The appropriate voucher depends on the transaction being recorded.

Can I learn TallyPrime without an accounting background?

Yes. You can begin with accounting fundamentals such as ledgers, debit, credit and basic transactions before progressing to TallyPrime exercises.

Do GST entries require separate tax ledgers?

GST transactions may involve the relevant tax ledgers and configured GST details. The exact entry depends on the transaction, applicable tax treatment and software configuration.

How can I check whether an entry is correct?

Verify the transaction details, ledgers, debit and credit amounts, tax treatment and voucher type. Review the relevant ledger and accounting reports afterwards.
