How to Check Day Book in TallyPrime: Step-by-Step Guide

Learn how to open the Day Book in TallyPrime, review daily transactions, filter vouchers by date, verify entries, and identify accounting mistakes.

INTRODUCTION

Introduction

The Day Book in TallyPrime is an important accounting report that helps businesses, accountants, and accounting students review transactions recorded during a particular period. It provides a consolidated view of accounting vouchers, including sales, purchases, receipts, payments, and journal entries.

Whether you are learning TallyPrime for the first time or working as an accountant, knowing how to check the Day Book helps you verify daily transactions, identify incorrect entries, and maintain accurate accounting records.

TallyPrime allows users to view Day Book transactions, select a particular date or period, open individual vouchers, and examine transaction details.

In this guide, you will learn how to open the Day Book in TallyPrime, filter transactions by date, understand the report, check voucher details, and troubleshoot common issues.

BASICS

What Is a Day Book in TallyPrime?

A Day Book is an accounting report that displays the vouchers entered into a company during a selected period. It helps users review the transactions recorded in the accounting system.

Example

If a business records five sales invoices, three purchase invoices, two payment vouchers, and four receipt vouchers in one day, the Day Book helps the accountant review those transactions in one place.

The Day Book is useful for monitoring daily accounting activities and checking whether transactions have been recorded correctly.

What Information Does the Day Book Show?

Depending on the report configuration and transaction type, the Day Book can display:

  • Date of the transaction.
  • Voucher type.
  • Voucher number.
  • Particulars or ledger details.
  • Debit and credit amounts.
  • Narration or transaction description.
  • Voucher details available for further inspection.

The information displayed may vary depending on the report settings, voucher type, and TallyPrime version.

Why Is the Day Book Important in Accounting?

The Day Book is useful for businesses and accounting professionals because it provides a convenient way to review recorded transactions.

  1. Daily transaction monitoring: Review the vouchers entered on a particular day.
  2. Error identification: Identify incorrect amounts, duplicate-looking entries, or unexpected vouchers.
  3. Voucher verification: Open a transaction to check its details.
  4. Accounting control: Help ensure transactions are recorded and classified appropriately.
  5. Audit preparation: Provide a useful starting point for reviewing transaction records.
  6. Bookkeeping practice: Help accounting students understand how different voucher types appear in accounting reports.

Checking the Day Book regularly can help businesses identify issues before they affect financial statements or GST reconciliation. You can also learn how voucher entries are classified in our guide to TallyPrime voucher types.

STEP-BY-STEP

How to Check Day Book in TallyPrime

Follow these steps to access the Day Book report.

01

Step 1: Open TallyPrime

Launch TallyPrime on your computer and select the company whose transactions you want to review.

Make sure the correct company is selected, especially if you maintain multiple company accounts.

02

Step 2: Open the Day Book Report

From the main screen, open the Day Book report.

Gateway of Tally Display More Reports Day Book

You may also be able to locate the report through the search or Go To feature by searching for Day Book.

The exact navigation may vary depending on your TallyPrime release and screen configuration.

03

Step 3: View the Transactions

Once the Day Book opens, TallyPrime displays the transactions recorded for the selected period.

Review the available details, including:

  • Transaction date.
  • Voucher type.
  • Voucher number.
  • Ledger particulars.
  • Transaction amount.

If you want to examine a particular transaction, select the relevant voucher and open it to review the details.

04

Step 4: Select a Specific Date

If you want to check transactions recorded on a particular day, change the report period.

For example, suppose you want to review all transactions recorded on 14 October 2026.

  1. Open the Day Book.
  2. Use the period or date-selection option.
  3. Enter the required start date.
  4. Enter the same date as the end date.
  5. Confirm the selection.

The report should display the transactions within the selected date range, subject to the report configuration.

05

Step 5: Check Transactions for a Date Range

You can also review transactions for an entire week, month, or another accounting period.

For example, to review transactions from 1 October 2026 to 14 October 2026, set the start and end dates accordingly.

This helps accountants review daily activity, compare entries across dates, and investigate transactions recorded during a particular period.

06

Step 6: Open and Verify a Voucher

If you notice an unfamiliar or incorrect-looking transaction, open the corresponding voucher.

Check the following details:

  • Voucher date and number.
  • Ledger accounts selected.
  • Debit and credit amounts.
  • Tax details, where applicable.
  • Narration.
  • Supporting invoice or transaction information.

If you identify an error, make corrections according to your company's accounting procedures and applicable controls.

Important: Do not modify or delete an entry merely because it looks unusual. Verify the source document and transaction details first.

Practical Example: Understanding Day Book Entries

Consider a small business that records the following transactions on a single day.

Transaction Voucher Type Amount
Cash sales Sales ₹5,000
Office stationery purchased on credit Purchase ₹1,000
Payment of electricity bill Payment ₹2,000
Cash received from a customer Receipt ₹3,000

When you open the Day Book for that date, these transactions can be reviewed through their respective vouchers.

The Day Book helps you identify which transactions were entered and open each voucher to check the corresponding ledger accounts and amounts.

Remember: These amounts illustrate the transactions only. They are not intended to represent a complete trial balance or a reconciliation of the business's cash and bank balances.

How to Check Different Voucher Types in the Day Book

The Day Book can help you review several types of accounting transactions.

1. Sales Vouchers

Review sales invoices and verify the customer ledger, taxable value, GST details where applicable, and total invoice amount.

2. Purchase Vouchers

Check purchase invoices, supplier details, expense or stock ledgers, and applicable tax information.

3. Payment Vouchers

Review payments made through cash or bank accounts, including expense payments and supplier settlements.

4. Receipt Vouchers

Check money received from customers, cash receipts, and other recorded receipts.

5. Journal Vouchers

Review adjustment entries, provisions, transfers, and other journal transactions.

Selecting and opening the appropriate voucher allows you to examine the accounting details in more depth.

For a fuller explanation of transaction entry types, read TallyPrime Voucher Types Explained.

How to Identify Accounting Errors Using the Day Book

The Day Book can help you identify transactions that require further investigation.

Incorrect Transaction Date

A voucher may have been recorded under the wrong date. Check the transaction date against the original invoice or supporting document.

Wrong Ledger Selection

An expense may have been posted to an incorrect ledger, or a receipt may have been recorded against the wrong customer. Open the voucher and verify the accounts used.

Incorrect Amount

Compare the recorded amount with the original invoice, receipt, or payment record.

Duplicate-Looking Entries

Two similar vouchers may appear in the Day Book. Verify their voucher numbers, supporting documents, and transaction details before deciding whether a duplicate exists.

Missing Transactions

If an expected transaction does not appear, check the selected date range, company, voucher status, and report configuration.

The Day Book is a useful checking tool, but it does not independently confirm that every transaction is complete or correct. Reconcile it with supporting documents and other accounting reports. For bank-related checks, see how to perform Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime.

Day Book vs Ledger Report in TallyPrime

Although both reports help accountants review transactions, they serve different purposes.

Feature Day Book Ledger Report
Main purpose Review vouchers recorded during a period Review transactions associated with a particular ledger
Scope Multiple voucher types and accounts One selected ledger account
Typical use Daily transaction checking Account-wise transaction verification
Example Review all vouchers recorded on a day Review all entries in the Bank Account ledger
Error checking Identify transactions needing investigation Verify account-specific postings and balances

For example, use the Day Book to review all transactions entered on a particular day. Use the ledger report to investigate the detailed activity of a specific customer, supplier, expense, cash, or bank account.

Both reports are useful for maintaining accurate accounting records.

Common Problems When Checking Day Book in TallyPrime

1. Day Book Shows No Transactions

Possible reasons:

  • The wrong company is selected.
  • The report period does not include the transaction date.
  • No vouchers have been entered for the selected period.
  • The report configuration is filtering the transactions.

Solution: Verify the company, date range, and available report filters.

2. A Transaction Is Missing

Possible reasons:

  • The voucher date is incorrect.
  • The transaction belongs to another company.
  • The voucher is not recorded as expected.
  • A report filter excludes the transaction.

Solution: Check the voucher date, company, and report configuration. Search for the voucher separately if necessary.

3. The Day Book Displays Too Many Transactions

Possible reason: The report period covers a longer duration than required.

Solution: Narrow the period to a specific date, week, or month.

4. You Cannot Find a Particular Voucher

Possible reasons:

  • You are searching under the wrong voucher type.
  • The voucher number or date is incorrect.
  • You are viewing the wrong company or period.

Solution: Verify the voucher details and use the available search, navigation, or filtering options.

5. You Need to Print or Export the Day Book

TallyPrime provides reporting and output options that may allow you to print or export reports.

Open the Day Book and check the available printing or export options in your version. Review the date range and displayed information before producing the report.

Best Practices for Checking the Day Book

Follow these practices to improve your daily accounting process:

  • Check the Day Book regularly.
  • Select the correct company before reviewing transactions.
  • Verify the date range before drawing conclusions.
  • Open vouchers that require further investigation.
  • Compare transactions with supporting documents.
  • Review ledger classifications and amounts.
  • Investigate duplicate-looking entries before changing them.
  • Use ledger reports and other financial reports when additional verification is required.
  • Follow your organisation's approval and recordkeeping procedures.

Consistent Day Book checking helps accountants maintain better visibility over recorded transactions.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I open the Day Book in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant company, locate the Day Book through the available report navigation or Go To search, and select the report to view the recorded vouchers.

2. What is the shortcut to open the Day Book in TallyPrime?

The exact shortcut or navigation may vary by TallyPrime version and configuration. The Go To or report search feature is a practical way to locate the Day Book when you do not know the navigation path.

3. Can I check the Day Book for one day only?

Yes. Use the report's date or period selection to set the start and end dates to the same day.

4. What transactions appear in the Day Book?

Depending on the report configuration, the Day Book can display sales, purchases, receipts, payments, journal entries, and other recorded vouchers.

5. What is the difference between a Day Book and a ledger?

The Day Book provides a broader view of vouchers recorded during a period. A ledger report focuses on the transactions and balances associated with a particular account.

6. Can I edit a voucher from the Day Book?

In supported workflows, you can open a voucher from the report and make changes if you have the required access and permissions. Follow your organisation's accounting controls before modifying saved transactions.

7. Can I print the Day Book in TallyPrime?

Yes. Depending on the version and available options, you can use the report's print or export features to produce a copy of the Day Book.

8. Why is the Day Book important for accounting students?

It helps students understand how sales, purchase, payment, receipt, and journal vouchers appear in an accounting system. It also provides practical experience in transaction verification and bookkeeping.

CONCLUSION

Conclusion

The Day Book in TallyPrime is a valuable report for reviewing accounting transactions recorded during a selected period. By learning how to open the report, select dates, review voucher details, and investigate unusual entries, users can improve their understanding of day-to-day accounting.

For accounting students and aspiring accountants, practising Day Book verification alongside ledger reports, trial balance reports, and voucher entry exercises helps build practical TallyPrime skills.
