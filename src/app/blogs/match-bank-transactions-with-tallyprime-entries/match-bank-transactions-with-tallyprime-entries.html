GETTING STARTED

Introduction

Matching bank transactions with accounting entries is an important task for accountants, business owners, and finance professionals. Every business needs to ensure that the transactions recorded in its accounting software match the transactions appearing in its bank statement.

Sometimes, the bank balance shown in TallyPrime may differ from the balance shown by the bank. This can happen because of unrecorded transactions, bank charges, cheque clearance delays, direct deposits, incorrect entries, or other timing differences.

TallyPrime helps users compare recorded bank transactions with bank statement details through its bank reconciliation features. By learning how to match bank transactions with TallyPrime entries, accounting students and working professionals can improve their reconciliation skills and maintain accurate financial records.

In this guide, you will learn how bank transaction matching works, how to reconcile bank entries in TallyPrime, how to identify mismatches, and how to practise bank reconciliation using a real-world example.

Key idea

Matching connects a bank statement transaction with its corresponding accounting entry. Reconciliation also investigates unmatched items and explains differences between the two sets of records.

THE BASICS

What Does Matching Bank Transactions With TallyPrime Entries Mean?

Matching bank transactions means comparing transactions recorded in TallyPrime with the transactions shown in the corresponding bank statement.

For example, a business records a supplier payment of ₹15,000 in TallyPrime. The same payment appears in the bank statement. Matching these two transactions confirms that the accounting record corresponds to the bank transaction.

This process is closely associated with bank reconciliation, which helps explain differences between the bank balance in the books and the balance reported by the bank.

Why Is Bank Transaction Matching Important?

Matching bank transactions helps businesses:

01

Verify transactions

Confirm that payments and receipts have been recorded correctly.

02

Find missing entries

Identify missing or duplicate accounting entries.

03

Check bank charges

Find bank charges and interest not yet recorded in TallyPrime.

04

Track cheques

Track cheques issued but not yet cleared and deposits not yet credited.

05

Detect errors

Identify incorrect amounts or transaction dates.

06

Support reporting

Maintain reliable cash and bank balances for month-end reporting.

Regular reconciliation helps accountants identify discrepancies early instead of waiting until the end of the financial year.

What Is Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime?

Bank reconciliation is the process of comparing the bank transactions recorded in the company's books with the transactions shown in the bank statement.

TallyPrime provides bank reconciliation facilities that help users review bank transactions, enter or import bank statement details where supported, and match transactions using the available reconciliation workflow.

Depending on the TallyPrime version, bank configuration, and available bank statement format, some steps may be automated while others may require manual review.

Remember: The main objective is to ensure that transactions are correctly recorded and that differences between the books and the bank statement are understood.

For a separate detailed walkthrough, read How to Perform Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime.

What You Need Before Matching Bank Transactions

Before starting bank reconciliation in TallyPrime, keep the following information ready:

  1. Your company's TallyPrime data.
  2. The relevant bank ledger.
  3. The bank statement for the reconciliation period.
  4. Recorded payment and receipt vouchers.
  5. Cheque numbers, transaction dates, and reference numbers, where applicable.
  6. Bank charges, interest entries, and other bank-generated transactions.
  7. Details of pending or uncleared transactions.

It is also helpful to select a specific reconciliation period, such as a month or financial quarter, to make the process easier to manage.

Review the relevant TallyPrime voucher types if you need to confirm which voucher is used for a receipt, payment, or adjustment.

PRACTICAL WORKFLOW

How to Match Bank Transactions With TallyPrime Entries: Step-by-Step Guide

Follow these steps to reconcile bank transactions in TallyPrime. The exact navigation and available options may vary with the release, bank configuration, and statement format.

01

Open Your Company in TallyPrime

Launch TallyPrime and open the company whose bank transactions you want to reconcile. Ensure that you are working with the correct company and accounting period before making changes.

02

Open the Bank Ledger

Locate the bank ledger associated with the account you want to reconcile. If the business maintains multiple bank accounts, reconcile each account separately using its corresponding statement.

03

Open the Bank Reconciliation Report

Navigate to the bank reconciliation report for the relevant bank ledger. In many TallyPrime configurations, you can access reconciliation through the bank ledger or the banking reports menu. Review the transactions displayed for the selected period.

04

Obtain the Bank Statement

Download or obtain the bank statement for the same period covered by the reconciliation report. Check the transaction date, debit or credit amount, description, cheque or reference number, bank transaction ID where available, and closing balance. Confirm that it belongs to the correct account and date range.

05

Import or Enter Bank Statement Details

If your TallyPrime version and bank support a compatible statement-import workflow, import the statement using the available banking features. Review the imported data to ensure dates, amounts, and references have been interpreted correctly.

If importing is unavailable or the statement format is unsupported, use the reconciliation screen to enter or update the relevant bank dates and details manually.

Important: Importing bank statement data does not automatically mean every transaction has been matched or every accounting entry has been created. Review the results before accepting changes.
06

Match Bank Transactions With Accounting Entries

Compare the transactions in the bank statement with the entries recorded in TallyPrime. Check the amount, date, payment or receipt description, cheque number, bank reference or transaction ID, and whether the transaction is a debit or credit.

For example, if TallyPrime contains a payment of ₹12,000 and the bank statement shows the same payment with a matching reference, identify the two records as corresponding transactions and use the available reconciliation options to mark or confirm the match.

If the date differs slightly because of bank processing, verify the amount and reference before marking it as reconciled.

07

Identify Unmatched Transactions

Some bank statement transactions may not have a corresponding entry in TallyPrime. Examples include bank service charges, interest credited by the bank, direct customer deposits, loan instalments or bank deductions, incorrectly recorded transactions, and duplicate or missing vouchers.

Investigate each unmatched item before deciding whether a new accounting voucher or a correction is required.

08

Record Missing Bank Transactions

If the bank statement contains a genuine transaction that has not been recorded in TallyPrime, create the appropriate accounting voucher after verifying the transaction.

For example, if the bank has charged a service fee of ₹250 that is missing from the books, the accounting entry may be:

Bank Charges Entry
Particulars Debit Credit
Bank Charges A/c ₹250 —
Bank A/c — ₹250
Total ₹250 ₹250

This records the bank charge as an expense and reduces the book balance. After recording the transaction, return to the reconciliation report and match it with the corresponding bank statement item.

09

Review Unreconciled Entries

Some transactions may remain unmatched because of timing differences. For example, a cheque issued to a supplier may be recorded in TallyPrime before it appears as a debit in the bank statement.

Do not delete or recreate such entries without investigation. Check the transaction status and supporting documents, and leave genuine outstanding items unreconciled until the bank processes them.

10

Verify the Reconciliation Results

Once matching is complete, review the bank reconciliation report. Confirm that matching transactions have been reconciled correctly, bank charges and other missing transactions have been recorded, unmatched items have been investigated, outstanding cheques and deposits are properly identified, and resulting balances and reconciliation dates are reasonable.

Save or retain the relevant reconciliation information according to your company's accounting procedures.

LEARN WITH AN EXAMPLE

Practical Example: Matching Bank Transactions in TallyPrime

Consider ABC Enterprises, which wants to reconcile its bank transactions for October. The following entries appear in TallyPrime and the bank statement.

Transaction TallyPrime Entry Bank Statement Result
Customer receipt ₹20,000 ₹20,000 Matched
Supplier payment ₹12,000 ₹12,000 Matched
Bank charges Not recorded ₹250 debit Record entry, then match
Cheque issued ₹8,000 Not yet cleared Outstanding
Interest credited Not recorded ₹500 credit Record entry, then match

How Should These Transactions Be Handled?

01

Customer receipt of ₹20,000

The amount appears in both records. Verify the reference and mark the transaction as reconciled.

02

Supplier payment of ₹12,000

The transaction appears in TallyPrime and the bank statement. Confirm the details and reconcile it.

03

Bank charges of ₹250

The bank has deducted the fee, but TallyPrime does not contain the corresponding expense. Record the bank charges using the appropriate voucher and then reconcile the transaction.

04

Outstanding cheque of ₹8,000

The cheque has been recorded in TallyPrime but has not yet been cleared by the bank. Investigate the cheque status and retain it as an outstanding item until the bank processes it.

05

Interest credited of ₹500

The bank has credited interest that is missing from the books. Record the appropriate interest income entry, considering the nature of the account and applicable accounting treatment, and then reconcile it.

What this example teaches

Bank reconciliation involves more than simply matching amounts. Accountants must identify timing differences, record missing transactions, and investigate discrepancies.

Common Reasons Bank Transactions Do Not Match

01

Different Transaction Dates

A transaction may be recorded in TallyPrime on one date but processed by the bank on another date.

Solution: Compare the transaction reference and amount, verify the bank processing date, and reconcile using the appropriate bank date.
02

Missing Bank Charges

Banks may deduct service fees, transaction charges, or other amounts without the business immediately recording them in its books.

Solution: Review the statement and record the appropriate expense entry.
03

Direct Deposits or Credits

A customer may deposit money directly into the company's bank account before the accounting team records the receipt.

Solution: Identify the source of the deposit, verify the supporting details, and record the receipt against the correct customer or account.
04

Duplicate Accounting Entries

The same payment or receipt may have been recorded twice in TallyPrime.

Solution: Check the voucher history, bank references, and supporting documents before correcting the duplicate.
05

Incorrect Transaction Amounts

The amount in the accounting voucher may differ from the bank statement because of a data-entry error or an incorrect transaction value.

Solution: Verify the original document and correct the entry through the appropriate accounting process.
06

Uncleared Cheques

Cheques issued or deposited may take time to appear in the bank statement.

Solution: Track the cheque status and reconcile it when the bank processes the transaction.
07

Incorrect Bank Ledger Selection

A transaction may be recorded against the wrong bank ledger.

Solution: Check the voucher details and confirm that the transaction belongs to the bank account being reconciled.

Best Practices for Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime

To maintain accurate financial records, follow these practices:

  • Reconcile bank transactions regularly, preferably every month or more frequently for high-volume accounts.
  • Use the correct bank statement for each account.
  • Keep bank references and transaction descriptions accurate.
  • Record bank charges, interest, and direct credits promptly.
  • Investigate unmatched transactions instead of ignoring them.
  • Avoid creating duplicate vouchers for transactions already recorded.
  • Track outstanding cheques and deposits.
  • Review reconciliation reports before finalising monthly accounts.
  • Restrict access to accounting data and maintain appropriate backups.
  • Retain bank statements and supporting documents for future reference.

A consistent reconciliation process helps businesses maintain reliable financial information and reduces the time needed to investigate accounting errors.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I match bank transactions in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant company, access the bank ledger and bank reconciliation report, compare the accounting entries with the bank statement, and mark matching transactions using the available reconciliation workflow. Investigate unmatched items and record missing transactions where necessary.

2. What is the difference between bank reconciliation and bank transaction matching?

Bank transaction matching identifies which bank statement transactions correspond to entries in the accounting books. Bank reconciliation is the broader process of matching those transactions, recording missing items, identifying timing differences, and explaining differences between balances.

3. Can I import a bank statement into TallyPrime?

Supported TallyPrime versions and banking configurations may allow bank statement import. Availability and compatibility depend on the statement format, bank, and software version. Check the available import options in your installation.

4. Why does my TallyPrime bank balance differ from my bank statement?

Differences may arise from uncleared cheques, deposits in transit, unrecorded bank charges, interest credits, missing vouchers, duplicate entries, or incorrect transaction details.

5. How do I record bank charges in TallyPrime?

Create or use an appropriate Bank Charges expense ledger and record the bank deduction through the suitable accounting voucher. For example, debit Bank Charges and credit the relevant Bank ledger. Then reconcile the transaction with the bank statement.

6. What should I do if a bank transaction is missing in TallyPrime?

Verify the transaction against the bank statement and supporting documents. If it is a genuine transaction that has not been recorded, create the appropriate voucher and then reconcile it.

7. How often should a business reconcile its bank account?

Many businesses reconcile monthly. Businesses with frequent transactions or stricter internal controls may reconcile weekly or daily. The appropriate frequency depends on transaction volume, risk, and reporting requirements.

8. Is bank reconciliation important for accounting jobs?

Yes. Bank reconciliation is a practical accounting skill used to verify transactions, identify errors, maintain accurate balances, and support financial reporting. Accounting students should practise it alongside payment vouchers, receipt vouchers, ledger management, and GST accounting.

Conclusion

Matching bank transactions with TallyPrime entries is an essential part of maintaining accurate accounting records. By comparing bank statements with accounting vouchers, recording missing transactions, investigating discrepancies, and reviewing reconciliation reports, accountants can keep financial information reliable and up to date.

For beginners, the best way to learn bank reconciliation is to practise with sample bank statements and accounting transactions. Understanding why a transaction matches, remains outstanding, or requires correction is more valuable than simply marking entries as reconciled.
