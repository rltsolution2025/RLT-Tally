How to View Balance Sheet in TallyPrime: Step-by-Step Guide

Learn how to open the Balance Sheet, select a reporting date, understand assets and liabilities, drill down into balances, and export the report.

Practical Accounting Guide Beginner Friendly

01 · Getting started

Introduction

A balance sheet is one of the most important financial reports used by businesses to understand their financial position. It provides a snapshot of a company's assets, liabilities, and capital or equity as of a particular date.

TallyPrime makes it easier for business owners, accountants, and accounting students to view financial reports, review ledger balances, and understand a company's financial position.

If you are learning accounting or preparing for an accountant job, knowing how to view a balance sheet in TallyPrime is an essential practical skill.

In this guide, you will learn how to open a balance sheet in TallyPrime, change the reporting date, understand assets and liabilities, drill down into account balances, and export the report for further analysis.

02 · The basics

What Is a Balance Sheet in TallyPrime?

A balance sheet in TallyPrime is a financial report that displays a company's assets, liabilities, and capital or equity at a specific point in time.

It helps users understand what the business owns, what it owes, and the funds attributable to its owners.

THE ACCOUNTING EQUATION Assets = Liabilities + Equity

For example, if a business has total assets of ₹10,00,000 and liabilities of ₹4,00,000, its equity would be ₹6,00,000, assuming the figures are prepared on a consistent accounting basis.

TallyPrime generates the balance sheet using the accounting transactions recorded in the selected company. The accuracy of the report depends on the completeness and correctness of the underlying accounting entries and the company's configuration.

04 · Step-by-step tutorial

How to View Balance Sheet in TallyPrime

Follow these steps to open the balance sheet in TallyPrime.

01
Step 1

Open TallyPrime

Launch TallyPrime on your computer. Select the company for which you want to view the balance sheet. If multiple companies are available, verify that you have opened the correct company before proceeding.

02
Step 2

Open the Balance Sheet Report

From the Gateway of Tally, select Balance Sheet.

GATEWAY OF TALLY Balance Sheet

In versions that support the relevant navigation, use Gateway of Tally → Balance Sheet. The report will display the financial balances for the selected company as of the reporting date. The precise menu layout may vary slightly depending on your TallyPrime release and screen configuration.

03
Step 3

Review the Balance Sheet

After opening the report, review the balances displayed under the relevant groups. Common sections include:

  • Capital account
  • Reserves and surplus
  • Loans and borrowings
  • Sundry creditors
  • Fixed assets
  • Current assets
  • Cash and bank balances
  • Stock or inventory balances

The exact group names and report presentation depend on your ledger structure, accounting configuration, and TallyPrime version.

04
Step 4

Change the Reporting Date

You may need to view the balance sheet for a particular financial year or date. Use the available period or report-date controls to select the required reporting date.

Example

To view the balance sheet as of 31 March 2026, enter that date in the report's date settings.

Remember that a balance sheet represents the financial position as of a date, rather than showing income and expenses over a period.

05
Step 5

Drill Down into Balances

Select the relevant group or ledger and open its detailed report to understand how a particular figure was calculated. You may drill down into sundry creditors, cash and bank balances, fixed assets, loans, or stock and inventory groups.

Drilling down helps you investigate account balances without leaving the reporting workflow.

06
Step 6

Review and Verify the Figures

Before using the balance sheet for management decisions or formal reporting, check that:

  • All relevant transactions have been entered.
  • Ledger groups are assigned correctly.
  • Bank balances have been reconciled where appropriate.
  • Inventory records are accurate.
  • Outstanding receivables and payables are reviewed.
  • Depreciation, provisions, and other required adjustments have been considered.
  • The reporting date and accounting period are correct.

Review the report alongside other relevant financial statements and supporting records. See our guide on generating a Trial Balance in TallyPrime for another important accounting verification step.

05 · Report configuration

How to Change the Balance Sheet Date in TallyPrime

Sometimes you may need to view a balance sheet for a previous date or financial year. For example, you may want to compare the company's financial position on 31 March 2025 with its position on 31 March 2026.

01
Open the required company

Confirm that you are working in the correct company.

02
Navigate to the Balance Sheet report

Open the report from the Gateway of Tally or the available navigation menu.

03
Use the date or period control

Locate the reporting date selection control available in that report.

04
Enter the required reporting date

Select the date that matches the financial information you want to review.

05
Confirm and review

Check the refreshed report and verify that the displayed date is correct.

Practical tip: Use the same reporting date when comparing two sets of financial information to avoid misleading comparisons. TallyPrime's exact key or control for changing the date can vary by report and version. Follow the on-screen instructions shown in your installation.

06 · Understand the figures

How to Understand the Balance Sheet in TallyPrime

Understanding the report is just as important as knowing how to open it.

01

Assets

Assets are resources controlled by a business that provide economic benefits.

Examples

  • Cash in hand
  • Bank balances
  • Inventory
  • Trade receivables
  • Furniture and equipment
  • Buildings and machinery

Assets may be classified as current or non-current, depending on the applicable accounting framework and presentation.

02

Liabilities

Liabilities are obligations that a business must settle.

Examples

  • Trade payables
  • Bank loans
  • Outstanding expenses
  • Other borrowings
  • Tax liabilities

Liabilities are generally classified as current or non-current based on the relevant reporting requirements.

03

Capital or Equity

Capital or equity represents the owner's interest in the business after deducting liabilities from assets. Depending on the business structure and accounting framework, this may include capital contributions, reserves, accumulated profits, and other equity balances.

For a sole proprietorship, the presentation of capital and drawings may differ from that of a company.

BALANCE SHEET FORMULA Assets = Liabilities + Equity

The equation shows how the resources of a business are financed through obligations and owners' funds.

07 · Apply what you learned

Practical Example: Understanding a Balance Sheet

Suppose ABC Traders has the following balances on 31 March 2026.

Particulars Amount
Cash and bank balances ₹1,00,000
Inventory ₹2,00,000
Trade receivables ₹1,50,000
Equipment ₹2,50,000
Total assets ₹7,00,000
Trade payables ₹1,00,000
Bank loan ₹2,00,000
Total liabilities ₹3,00,000
Owner's equity ₹4,00,000
THE ACCOUNTING EQUATION ₹7,00,000 = ₹3,00,000 + ₹4,00,000

Total assets = Total liabilities + Owner's equity

The balance sheet balances

The balance sheet balances because total assets equal total liabilities plus equity. This example is simplified for learning purposes. Actual financial statements may contain additional accounts, adjustments, and classifications.

08 · Sharing reports

How to Export or Print the Balance Sheet in TallyPrime

Businesses often need to share financial reports with management, accountants, auditors, or other authorised users. TallyPrime provides report output options that may include printing and exporting, depending on the version and configuration.

Steps to Print a Balance Sheet

  1. Open the Balance Sheet report.
  2. Select the required reporting date.
  3. Review the displayed figures.
  4. Open the report's print option.
  5. Check the print configuration.
  6. Preview the report if available.
  7. Print after verifying the details.

Steps to Export a Balance Sheet

  1. Open the Balance Sheet report.
  2. Set the required reporting date.
  3. Select the export option available in your version.
  4. Choose a supported format, such as PDF or Excel, if available.
  5. Review the export settings.
  6. Export and verify the generated file.

PDF is useful for sharing a fixed-layout financial report, while Excel can be useful for further analysis. Check the exported figures and formatting before distributing the file.

Security tip: A balance sheet contains confidential financial information. Share exported reports only with authorised people.

For a more detailed walkthrough, read How to Export TallyPrime Reports to Excel and PDF.

09 · Troubleshooting

Common Problems When Viewing a Balance Sheet in TallyPrime

01

Balance Sheet Shows Incorrect Figures

Possible causes: Transactions are missing or incorrectly recorded, ledgers are assigned to incorrect groups, adjustments have not been entered, or the wrong reporting date is selected.

Solution: Verify the underlying vouchers, ledger groups, adjustments, and report date.

02

Balance Sheet Does Not Match Expected Totals

A balance sheet may require investigation if the reported figures differ from expected accounting balances.

Solution: Review the underlying ledger balances and accounting entries. Confirm that the books are complete and required year-end adjustments have been recorded correctly. Do not assume every difference is caused by a software error.

03

Some Ledgers Are Missing from the Report

Possible causes: The ledger is grouped incorrectly, has no relevant balance, or report configuration affects how details appear.

Solution: Open the relevant ledger, check its group, and review the report's display configuration.

04

Balance Sheet Shows Data for the Wrong Date

Possible cause: The report is set to a different date than the one you intended to review.

Solution: Return to the report's date settings, enter the correct date, and review the refreshed balances.

05

Exported Balance Sheet Is Difficult to Read

Possible causes: Incorrect export format or layout settings, columns are too narrow, or the report is not formatted for the selected output.

Solution: Review the export configuration, choose an appropriate output format, and inspect the file before sharing it.

10 · Accounting quality

Best Practices for Accurate Balance Sheet Reporting

To improve the reliability of balance sheet reports in TallyPrime:

  • Record business transactions promptly.
  • Assign the correct groups to ledgers.
  • Reconcile bank accounts regularly.
  • Review outstanding receivables and payables.
  • Maintain accurate inventory records.
  • Record depreciation and other required adjustments.
  • Verify opening balances and year-end entries.
  • Use the correct reporting date.
  • Review the balance sheet alongside the profit and loss statement and supporting ledgers.
  • Keep backups and restrict access to confidential financial records.

Consistent accounting practices make balance sheet reports more useful for business planning and financial review. You can also read How to Read a Profit and Loss Statement in TallyPrime to understand the other major financial statement.

11 · Frequently asked questions

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I view the balance sheet in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant company, navigate to the Gateway of Tally, and select Balance Sheet. Review the report and choose the required reporting date.

2. What is the shortcut key to open the balance sheet in TallyPrime?

The most direct method is to select Balance Sheet from the Gateway of Tally. Available navigation shortcuts can vary by TallyPrime version and configuration, so check the on-screen shortcut guidance in your installation.

3. Can I view a balance sheet for a previous financial year?

Yes. Use the report's date or period controls to select the required historical reporting date, provided the relevant accounting data is available.

4. How can I view the detailed ledger balances in a balance sheet?

Select the relevant group or ledger in the report and open the detailed view. This allows you to investigate the transactions and balances contributing to the reported figure.

5. Can I export the balance sheet from TallyPrime to Excel?

TallyPrime supports report-export workflows, and Excel may be available as an output format depending on your version and configuration. Review the export options displayed in your installation.

6. Why is the balance sheet important in accounting?

A balance sheet shows the financial position of a business at a particular date. It helps users understand assets, liabilities, and equity and supports financial review and decision-making.

7. What is the difference between a balance sheet and a profit and loss statement?

A balance sheet shows the financial position at a specific date. A profit and loss statement summarises income and expenses over a reporting period to determine the profit or loss.

8. Can beginners learn balance sheet preparation using TallyPrime?

Yes. Beginners can practise entering transactions, creating ledgers, viewing the balance sheet, and drilling down into account balances using a properly configured practice company.

12 · Key takeaway

Conclusion

Learning how to view a balance sheet in TallyPrime is an essential accounting skill for students, accountants, and business owners.

By opening the report, selecting the correct reporting date, reviewing assets and liabilities, drilling down into ledger balances, and exporting the report when required, you can develop a clearer understanding of a company's financial position.

For reliable reporting, remember that the accuracy of the balance sheet depends on correctly recorded transactions, appropriate ledger grouping, complete accounting records, and the necessary adjustments.
