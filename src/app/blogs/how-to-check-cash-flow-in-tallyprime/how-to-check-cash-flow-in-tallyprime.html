01 · Getting started

Introduction

Understanding how to check cash flow in TallyPrime is an important accounting skill for business owners, accountants, and students learning financial management. Cash flow helps businesses understand how money enters and leaves their accounts during a specific period.

TallyPrime provides financial reports that help users review cash and bank transactions, monitor receipts and payments, and analyse business finances. By learning how to access and interpret these reports, you can better understand a company's cash position and make informed financial decisions.

In this guide, you will learn how to check cash flow in TallyPrime, understand the difference between cash flow and cash balance, analyse receipts and payments, and use practical examples to improve your accounting knowledge.

02 · Accounting fundamentals

What Is Cash Flow in Accounting?

Cash flow refers to the movement of money into and out of a business over a particular period.

Cash inflows represent money received by the business, while cash outflows represent money paid by the business.

MONEY RECEIVED

Cash Inflows

Examples of money entering a business:

  • Cash received from customers.
  • Payments against outstanding invoices.
  • Capital introduced by the owner.
  • Loan proceeds received.
  • Other business receipts.
MONEY PAID

Cash Outflows

Examples of money leaving a business:

  • Payments to suppliers.
  • Employee salary payments.
  • Office rent and utility expenses.
  • Loan repayments.
  • Purchases of equipment and other assets.
Key takeaway: Monitoring cash flow helps businesses understand whether they have sufficient cash available to meet their financial obligations.

05 · Step-by-step tutorial

How to Check Cash Flow in TallyPrime: Step-by-Step Guide

Follow these steps to view the cash flow report in TallyPrime.

01
Step 1

Open TallyPrime

Launch TallyPrime and select the company for which you want to review cash flow.

Make sure you have selected the correct company and accounting period before opening the report.

02
Step 2

Open the Cash Flow Report

From the Gateway of Tally, navigate to the reports section and locate the Cash Flow report.

REPORT NAVIGATION
Gateway of Tally Financial Reports Cash Flow

Depending on your TallyPrime release, you may be able to access the report through the relevant financial reports menu or use the report search feature to locate Cash Flow.

Open the report to view the available cash flow information.

03
Step 3

Select the Required Period

Choose the period you want to analyse, such as:

  • A particular day.
  • A week.
  • A month.
  • A quarter.
  • A financial year.
  • A custom date range.
Example

To review cash movement for October 2026, select the relevant start and end dates for that month.

Selecting the correct period is important because the report's figures depend on the dates chosen.

04
Step 4

Review Cash Inflows

Examine the transactions that contribute to cash inflows.

These may include:

  • Customer receipts.
  • Capital contributions.
  • Loan receipts.
  • Other transactions that increase cash or cash equivalents.

Review the relevant ledger balances and underlying vouchers if you need to verify an amount.

05
Step 5

Review Cash Outflows

Next, examine transactions that represent cash outflows.

Common examples include:

  • Supplier payments.
  • Salary payments.
  • Rent and utility bills.
  • Payments for business assets.

Check the related payment vouchers and cash or bank ledgers if you need more information about a transaction.

06
Step 6

Analyse the Net Cash Movement

Net cash movement is the difference between cash inflows and cash outflows for the period.

CASH FLOW FORMULA Net Cash Movement = Total Cash Inflows − Total Cash Outflows

For example, if a business receives ₹1,50,000 and pays ₹1,10,000 during a period:

Particulars Amount
Total cash inflows ₹1,50,000
Total cash outflows ₹1,10,000
Net cash movement ₹40,000
NET CASH MOVEMENT + ₹40,000

The business has a positive net cash movement.

Positive cash movement does not necessarily mean that the business is profitable. Cash flow and accounting profit measure different things.
07
Step 7

Verify the Cash and Bank Balances

After reviewing the cash flow report, check the Cash/Bank Book or relevant ledger reports.

Verify the opening balance, receipts, payments, and closing balance for the selected period.

CASH BALANCE FORMULA Closing Cash Balance = Opening Cash Balance + Cash Receipts − Cash Payments

For example:

Particulars Amount
Opening cash balance ₹25,000
Cash receipts ₹50,000
Cash payments ₹35,000
Closing cash balance ₹40,000

This calculation helps you understand how the available cash balance changed during the period.

Remember that the cash balance is not the same as the overall cash flow report, which may present broader information about cash movement.

07 · Understand the difference

Difference Between Cash Flow and Cash Balance in TallyPrime

Cash flow and cash balance are related, but they are not the same.

Feature Cash Flow Cash Balance
Meaning Movement of cash during a period Cash available at a particular point in time
Focus Receipts and payments over time Available cash at a date
Purpose Understand cash movement Know how much cash remains
Example Net cash movement of ₹30,000 in October Closing cash balance of ₹1,10,000 on 31 October

Understanding this difference helps you interpret financial reports correctly.

08 · Financial analysis

Difference Between Cash Flow and Profit

A business may earn a profit but still experience a shortage of cash.

For example, a company may sell goods worth ₹1,00,000 on credit. The sale may contribute to accounting revenue, but the business does not receive the cash until the customer pays.

Similarly, a business may repay a loan principal using cash without treating that principal repayment as an ordinary operating expense.

Cash Flow Profit
Tracks cash movement Measures income minus expenses under accounting rules
Includes receipts and payments that affect cash Includes revenues and expenses recognised under accrual accounting
Helps assess liquidity Helps assess financial performance
Can be positive even when a business reports a loss Can be positive even when cash is temporarily insufficient
For a complete financial analysis, businesses should review cash flow alongside Profit and Loss reports and the Balance Sheet in TallyPrime .

10 · Troubleshooting

Common Problems When Checking Cash Flow in TallyPrime

01

Cash Flow Report Is Not Displaying Expected Figures

Possible reasons:

  • The wrong accounting period has been selected.
  • Transactions have not been entered or saved.
  • Relevant ledgers are configured incorrectly.
  • The report includes or excludes accounts differently than expected.

Solution: Verify the selected period, transaction vouchers, ledger configuration, and report settings.

02

Cash Balance Does Not Match the Physical Cash Available

Possible reasons:

  • A receipt or payment was missed.
  • A voucher was recorded incorrectly.
  • A transaction was entered into the wrong ledger.
  • The physical cash count has not been reconciled with the books.

Solution: Review the Cash Book, inspect the related vouchers, and reconcile the book balance with the actual cash available.

03

Bank Balance Does Not Match the Bank Statement

Possible reasons:

  • Cheques have not cleared.
  • Bank charges have not been recorded.
  • Certain transactions are missing.
  • The bank reconciliation process is incomplete.

Solution: Review the Bank Book and perform bank reconciliation using the relevant bank statement. Read our guide on Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime .

04

Cash Flow Appears Positive but the Business Has Payment Difficulties

Possible reasons:

  • Large payments are due after the selected period.
  • Some receipts are irregular or non-recurring.
  • Funds are tied up in receivables or inventory.
  • Loan repayments or major purchases are expected soon.

Solution: Review upcoming payment obligations, outstanding customer balances, and expected receipts before making financial decisions.

11 · Accounting practices

Best Practices for Cash Flow Management Using TallyPrime

Follow these practices to maintain better control over cash movement:

  • Record receipts and payments promptly.
  • Select the correct ledger for every transaction.
  • Review cash flow regularly.
  • Reconcile bank transactions with bank statements.
  • Monitor outstanding customer invoices.
  • Plan supplier payments and recurring expenses.
  • Compare actual cash movement with your budget.
  • Review cash flow alongside the Balance Sheet and Profit and Loss report.
  • Keep accounting records accurate and up to date.

Regular monitoring helps businesses identify potential cash shortages early and improve financial planning.

12 · Frequently asked questions

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I check cash flow in TallyPrime?

Open the required company, navigate to the Cash Flow report through the available financial reports menu or report search, select the required period, and review the displayed information. You can use the Cash/Bank Book and relevant ledgers to investigate individual transactions.

2. What is the shortcut key for the Cash Flow report in TallyPrime?

The shortcut can vary depending on the TallyPrime release, screen, and configuration. Use the report search feature or consult the shortcut information available in your installed version to locate Cash Flow.

3. Can I check monthly cash flow in TallyPrime?

Yes. Select the required monthly date range in the relevant report to analyse cash movement for that period.

4. What is the difference between the Cash Flow report and the Cash Book?

The Cash Flow report focuses on cash movement during a period, while the Cash Book records transactions posted to the cash ledger and helps track cash receipts, payments, and balances. Their exact presentation and scope differ.

5. Why is cash flow important for a business?

Cash flow helps a business understand whether it can meet its day-to-day payment obligations, manage operating expenses, and plan future spending.

6. Can a profitable business have negative cash flow?

Yes. A business may report profit while cash is tied up in unpaid customer invoices, inventory, or other working capital. Loan repayments and capital purchases can also affect cash without reducing profit in the same way.

7. Is the Cash Flow report the same as the Profit and Loss report?

No. The Cash Flow report focuses on cash movement, while the Profit and Loss report measures revenues and expenses over a period under accounting rules.

8. Can beginners learn cash flow management using TallyPrime?

Yes. Beginners can practise viewing financial reports, recording receipts and payments, reconciling balances, and understanding cash movement using a properly configured practice company.

For more complete financial analysis, review cash flow alongside the Cash/Bank Book, Balance Sheet, and Profit and Loss report.
