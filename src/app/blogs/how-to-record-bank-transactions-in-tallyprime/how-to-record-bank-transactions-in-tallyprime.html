01 / INTRODUCTION

Introduction

Recording bank transactions accurately is an essential part of accounting for every business. Companies receive customer payments, pay suppliers, transfer funds between accounts, and manage various banking activities daily. Maintaining accurate records helps businesses track their cash flow, monitor bank balances, and identify differences between accounting records and bank statements.

TallyPrime makes it easier to record banking transactions using accounting vouchers such as Receipt, Payment, Contra, and Journal. It also provides banking features that help users manage cheque details, review bank balances, and perform bank reconciliation.

Whether you are an accounting student, a business owner, or an aspiring accountant, learning how to record bank transactions in TallyPrime will help you develop practical accounting skills.

In this guide, you will learn how to create a bank ledger, record receipts and payments, transfer funds, enter cheque transactions, and reconcile bank statements in TallyPrime.

What you will learn

How to choose the appropriate voucher, record common bank transactions, understand the related debit and credit entries, and verify the records through TallyPrime reports.

02 / BASICS

What Are Bank Transactions in TallyPrime?

Bank transactions are financial activities that involve money entering or leaving a business's bank account.

Examples include:

Customer Receipts

Receiving payments from customers.

Supplier Payments

Paying suppliers and vendors.

Cash Deposits

Depositing cash into a bank account.

Cash Withdrawals

Withdrawing cash from a bank.

Bank Transfers

Transferring money between bank accounts.

Bank Adjustments

Recording bank charges and interest.

Businesses may also make payments through cheques, NEFT, RTGS, or IMPS. In TallyPrime, these transactions are recorded using the appropriate accounting vouchers. Selecting the correct voucher helps maintain accurate ledgers and financial reports.

03 / VOUCHER TYPES

Types of Bank Transactions in TallyPrime

Understanding the different voucher types is the first step towards recording bank transactions correctly. The following table explains the common vouchers and their uses.

Voucher Type Purpose Example
Receipt Records money received Customer pays ₹10,000 into the bank
Payment Records money paid Business pays a supplier ₹5,000
Contra Records cash and bank transfers Cash deposited into a bank account
Journal Records eligible accounting adjustments Bank interest accrued but not yet received
Purchase Records purchases that may be settled through a bank payment Purchase of office equipment
Sales Records sales that may be settled through a bank receipt Sale of goods to a customer

Important: The purchase and sales vouchers record the underlying transaction. The bank receipt or payment records the settlement when it occurs. Avoid recording the same income, expense, or liability twice. For a broader overview, read TallyPrime Voucher Types Explained with Examples.

05 / RECEIPT VOUCHER

Step 2: Record Money Received into the Bank

When a business receives money from a customer directly into its bank account, a Receipt voucher is commonly used to record the settlement.

Practical Example

ABC Enterprises receives ₹15,000 from Customer A through a bank transfer.

Accounting EntryReceipt Voucher
Particulars Debit Credit
HDFC Bank Account ₹15,000 —
Customer A — ₹15,000
Total ₹15,000 ₹15,000

The bank account is debited because the business's bank balance increases. Customer A is credited because the outstanding amount owed by the customer decreases.

Steps to Record the Receipt

  1. Open Vouchers in TallyPrime.
  2. Select the Receipt voucher.
  3. Choose the appropriate bank account.
  4. Select Customer A as the account from which the money is received.
  5. Enter the amount of ₹15,000.
  6. Add the bank reference, transaction date, or other relevant details where supported.
  7. Save the voucher.

The exact screen layout depends on your voucher mode and TallyPrime configuration.

When to use this entry: Use it when the customer payment is received and needs to be recorded in the bank ledger. If the sale was already recorded as a credit sale, the receipt settles the customer's outstanding balance instead of recording the sales income again. You can also read about tracking receivables and payables in TallyPrime.

06 / PAYMENT VOUCHER

Step 3: Record Money Paid from the Bank

A Payment voucher is commonly used when the business makes a payment from its bank account.

Practical Example

ABC Enterprises pays ₹8,000 for office rent through its bank account.

Accounting EntryPayment Voucher
Particulars Debit Credit
Office Rent ₹8,000 —
HDFC Bank Account — ₹8,000
Total ₹8,000 ₹8,000

The rent expense is debited because the expense increases. The bank account is credited because the bank balance decreases.

Steps to Record the Payment

  1. Open the Payment voucher.
  2. Select HDFC Bank Account.
  3. Choose the Office Rent ledger.
  4. Enter ₹8,000.
  5. Enter the payment method or bank reference details where applicable.
  6. Add a narration describing the transaction.
  7. Save the voucher.

Example narration

Office rent paid through HDFC Bank for the month.

If the rent was already recorded as an outstanding liability, select the relevant liability ledger instead of debiting the rent expense again.

07 / CONTRA VOUCHER

Step 4: Transfer Money Between Cash and Bank

Businesses frequently deposit cash into bank accounts or withdraw cash for office expenses. The Contra voucher is commonly used for transfers between cash and bank accounts or between eligible bank accounts.

Example 1: Deposit Cash into the Bank

ABC Enterprises deposits ₹20,000 in cash into its bank account.

Accounting EntryCash Deposit
Particulars Debit Credit
HDFC Bank Account ₹20,000 —
Cash — ₹20,000
Total ₹20,000 ₹20,000

The bank balance increases, while the cash balance decreases.

Example 2: Withdraw Cash from the Bank

The business withdraws ₹5,000 from its bank account for office use.

Accounting EntryCash Withdrawal
Particulars Debit Credit
Cash ₹5,000 —
HDFC Bank Account — ₹5,000
Total ₹5,000 ₹5,000

Steps to Record a Contra Entry

  1. Open the Contra voucher in TallyPrime.
  2. Select the relevant cash and bank ledgers.
  3. Enter the amount transferred.
  4. Verify the debit and credit accounts.
  5. Add a narration if required.
  6. Save the voucher.

A transfer between the company's own accounts is not income or an expense. It simply changes where the business's funds are held.

08 / BANK TRANSFER

Step 5: Record Bank-to-Bank Transfers

If a business transfers money from one bank account to another, a Contra voucher is generally used.

Practical Example

ABC Enterprises transfers ₹25,000 from HDFC Bank to ICICI Bank.

Accounting EntryBank-to-Bank Contra
Particulars Debit Credit
ICICI Bank Account ₹25,000 —
HDFC Bank Account — ₹25,000
Total ₹25,000 ₹25,000

Steps

  1. Open the Contra voucher.
  2. Select ICICI Bank as the receiving account.
  3. Select HDFC Bank as the sending account.
  4. Enter ₹25,000.
  5. Include the transaction reference if required.
  6. Save the voucher.

Verify both bank statements to confirm that the transfer was completed and recorded correctly.

09 / BANK CHARGES AND INTEREST

Steps 6 and 7: Record Bank Charges and Interest

Step 6: Record Bank Charges

Banks may deduct charges for account maintenance, payment processing, cheque services, or other banking activities.

Practical Example

The bank deducts ₹350 as bank charges.

Accounting EntryBank Charges
Particulars Debit Credit
Bank Charges ₹350 —
HDFC Bank Account — ₹350
Total ₹350 ₹350

How to Record Bank Charges

  1. Open the Payment voucher.
  2. Select HDFC Bank Account.
  3. Select the Bank Charges ledger.
  4. Enter ₹350.
  5. Add a narration.
  6. Save the voucher.

If GST is separately charged by the bank, account for it according to the bank document and the business's applicable GST and input tax credit eligibility.

Step 7: Record Bank Interest Received

Businesses may receive interest credited to their bank accounts.

Practical Example

The bank credits ₹600 as interest income.

Accounting EntryBank Interest
Particulars Debit Credit
HDFC Bank Account ₹600 —
Interest Income — ₹600
Total ₹600 ₹600

Steps

  1. Open the Receipt voucher.
  2. Select HDFC Bank Account.
  3. Select the Interest Income ledger.
  4. Enter ₹600.
  5. Add a narration explaining the interest credit.
  6. Save the voucher.

If the interest has already been accrued through an earlier journal entry, record the receipt against the appropriate accrued-interest ledger instead of crediting income a second time.

10 / CHEQUE ENTRIES

Step 8: Record Cheque Transactions

Businesses may receive or issue cheques for customer payments and supplier settlements. TallyPrime provides banking-related features to record cheque details and manage supported cheque workflows.

Example: Payment to a Supplier by Cheque

ABC Enterprises pays Supplier B ₹12,000 through a cheque.

Accounting EntrySupplier Cheque Payment
Particulars Debit Credit
Supplier B ₹12,000 —
HDFC Bank Account — ₹12,000
Total ₹12,000 ₹12,000

The supplier's outstanding liability decreases, and the bank balance is reduced according to the applicable accounting treatment and transaction status.

Steps

  1. Open the appropriate Payment voucher.
  2. Select the bank account.
  3. Select Supplier B.
  4. Enter ₹12,000.
  5. Enter cheque details using the available banking options.
  6. Save the voucher.

For cheques that have been issued but not yet cleared, use the appropriate cheque-management and reconciliation workflow in your TallyPrime configuration. Do not assume that the bank statement and book balance will change on the same date.

11 / RECONCILIATION

Step 9: Perform Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime

Bank reconciliation is the process of comparing the transactions recorded in the company's books with those appearing in the bank statement.

Differences can occur because of timing, bank charges, interest credits, unpresented cheques, deposits in transit, or missing entries.

Example

Suppose TallyPrime shows a bank balance of ₹50,000, but the bank statement shows ₹48,500. The difference is ₹1,500.

Difference to Investigate
₹50,000₹48,500
₹1,500

Compare the entries and supporting bank statement before posting an adjustment.

Before making an adjustment, investigate the reason. For example, an unrecorded bank charge of ₹1,500 could explain the difference, but the amount should be verified against the actual bank statement.

Steps to Reconcile the Bank

01

Open the Relevant Bank Ledger or Report

Open the relevant bank ledger or banking reports.

02

Access Bank Reconciliation

Access the Bank Reconciliation feature.

03

Select the Account and Period

Select the bank account and relevant period.

04

Compare Transactions

Compare the recorded transactions with the bank statement.

05

Enter or Confirm Bank Details

Enter or confirm bank dates and other reconciliation details as supported.

06

Investigate Unmatched Transactions

Identify unmatched transactions and investigate differences.

07

Record Verified Missing Entries

Record missing transactions, such as bank charges, when verified.

08

Review Reconciliation Status

Review the reconciliation status and retain the relevant bank statement for reference.

The exact menu path depends on the TallyPrime release and bank integration or statement-import configuration. Bank reconciliation does not mean changing balances simply to make them match. Identify and document the actual cause of every material difference.

13 / ACCOUNTING CHECKLIST

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Recording Bank Transactions

1. Selecting the Wrong Voucher

Using a Receipt, Payment, or Contra voucher incorrectly can affect account classification and reporting.

Solution: Identify the purpose of the transaction before selecting the voucher.

2. Recording Customer Receipts as New Sales

If a credit sale has already been recorded, recording the customer payment as sales income again can overstate revenue.

Solution: Record the receipt against the customer's outstanding balance.

3. Recording Supplier Payments as New Expenses

If a purchase or expense has already been recorded, paying the supplier should generally settle the outstanding liability.

Solution: Select the supplier or relevant payable ledger when recording the settlement.

4. Ignoring Bank Charges and Interest

Unrecorded bank charges or interest can create differences between the books and bank statement.

Solution: Review bank statements regularly and record verified transactions.

5. Skipping Bank Reconciliation

Without reconciliation, errors and missing entries may remain undetected.

Solution: Reconcile bank accounts regularly and investigate unexplained differences.

6. Entering Incorrect Transaction Dates

Incorrect dates can cause reporting and reconciliation problems.

Solution: Use the correct accounting date and bank transaction details, following your business's accounting policy.

15 / FAQS

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I record bank transactions in TallyPrime?

Create or verify the bank ledger, select the appropriate Receipt, Payment, Contra, or other relevant voucher, enter the transaction details, and save the entry. Review the bank ledger and relevant reports afterwards.

2. Which voucher is used for bank receipts in TallyPrime?

A Receipt voucher is commonly used to record money received into the bank, such as a customer payment or bank interest. Select the appropriate ledger based on the nature of the receipt.

3. Which voucher is used for bank payments?

A Payment voucher is commonly used for money paid from the bank, such as rent, supplier settlements, or bank charges.

4. What is a Contra voucher in TallyPrime?

A Contra voucher is generally used to record transfers between cash and bank accounts or between eligible bank accounts. Examples include cash deposits, cash withdrawals, and transfers between the company's own bank accounts.

5. How do I record bank charges in TallyPrime?

Create or select the Bank Charges expense ledger, open a Payment voucher, select the bank account and Bank Charges ledger, enter the amount, and save the voucher. Verify any separately charged GST where applicable.

6. How do I reconcile bank transactions in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant bank reconciliation feature, compare the book entries with the bank statement, mark or enter the relevant reconciliation details, and investigate unmatched transactions. Record missing entries only after verifying them.

7. Can I record NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, and UPI transactions in TallyPrime?

Yes. These transactions can generally be recorded using the appropriate accounting voucher and bank ledger. Include the relevant transaction reference and reconcile the entry with the bank statement.

8. Why does my bank balance differ from the bank statement?

Differences may arise from unrecorded bank charges, interest, outstanding cheques, deposits in transit, timing differences, or incorrect entries. Review the bank statement and reconciliation report to identify the actual cause.

9. Can beginners learn bank accounting in TallyPrime?

Yes. Beginners can practise bank receipts, payments, cash transfers, bank charges, interest entries, and reconciliation using a test company. Understanding the accounting logic behind each entry is as important as learning the software steps.

16 / CONCLUSION

Conclusion

Recording bank transactions in TallyPrime is an important accounting skill for managing business receipts, payments, transfers, and bank-related expenses. By using the correct vouchers, maintaining accurate bank ledgers, recording transaction references, and reconciling bank statements, businesses can improve the accuracy of their accounting records.

For accounting students and aspiring accountants, practising these entries provides a foundation for understanding cash flow, ledger balances, financial reports, and day-to-day business accounting.
