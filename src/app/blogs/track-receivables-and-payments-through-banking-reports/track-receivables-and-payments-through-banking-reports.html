INTRODUCTION

How to Track Receivables and Payments Through Banking Reports in TallyPrime

Tracking receivables and payments is an important part of managing business finances. Businesses need to know how much money customers owe, which invoices are overdue, whether payments have been received, and whether bank transactions match their accounting records.

TallyPrime helps businesses manage customer outstanding balances, record receipts and payments, review bank-related reports, and reconcile transactions. By regularly reviewing these reports, accountants can identify pending collections, monitor cash flow, and maintain accurate financial records.

In this guide, you will learn how to track receivables and payments through banking and accounting reports in TallyPrime, understand the difference between outstanding invoices and bank transactions, and practise the process using real-world examples.

Key point

Use receivables and payables reports to monitor party balances, the Bank Book to review recorded bank activity, and Bank Reconciliation to compare your books with the bank statement.

ACCOUNTING BASICS

What Are Receivables and Payments in Accounting?

Before exploring TallyPrime reports, it is important to understand the two concepts.

What Are Receivables?

Receivables are amounts a business expects to collect from customers for goods or services supplied on credit. For example, if a business sells goods worth ₹25,000 to a customer on credit, the customer owes ₹25,000 until the payment is received or the balance is otherwise settled.

Receivables help businesses understand how much money is pending from customers.

What Are Payments?

Payments are amounts transferred or paid to settle business obligations. These may include payments to suppliers, employees, service providers, or other parties. Businesses also record receipts when they receive money from customers.

In TallyPrime, receipt vouchers, payment vouchers, bank ledgers, and related reports help accountants monitor these transactions. Learn more about the available voucher categories in TallyPrime Voucher Types Explained.

01 / RECEIVABLES

Money expected from customers

Outstanding customer invoices and amounts due for credit sales.

02 / PAYMENTS

Money paid to settle obligations

Supplier, employee, service-provider, and other business payments.

REPORT GUIDE

Which TallyPrime Reports Help Track Receivables and Payments?

TallyPrime provides different reports for monitoring outstanding balances and banking transactions. Each report serves a different purpose.

Report Main Purpose
Bills Receivable or Receivables reports Track customer invoices and outstanding amounts.
Bills Payable or Payables reports Monitor outstanding supplier bills.
Day Book Review recorded receipts, payments, sales, and other vouchers.
Bank Book Review transactions recorded in a bank ledger.
Cash or Bank Summary Review relevant cash and bank balances.
Bank Reconciliation Compare recorded bank transactions with the bank statement.
Ledger Vouchers Review the transaction history of a customer, supplier, or bank ledger.
Important: Receivables reports tell you what customers owe according to your books. Banking reports show recorded bank activity. Bank reconciliation helps you compare those records with actual bank statement transactions. Report names and navigation may vary by TallyPrime release and configuration.
CUSTOMER COLLECTIONS

How to Track Receivables in TallyPrime

Follow these steps to review outstanding customer balances and identify overdue invoices.

01

Open Your Company in TallyPrime

Open TallyPrime and select the company whose receivables you want to review. Make sure sales invoices and customer receipts have been recorded correctly.

02

Open the Receivables Report

Navigate to the relevant outstanding statements or receivables report using the available report menus. Depending on your version, you may find this under the Statements of Accounts or outstanding reports area. Select the customer outstanding or bills receivable view.

03

Review Customer Outstanding Balances

The report can help you identify customer names, outstanding invoice references, pending amounts, bill dates and due dates where configured, and ageing information where available.

04

Check Overdue Invoices

Use the available ageing or due-date options to identify invoices that have remained unpaid beyond their expected payment dates. For example, if a customer was expected to pay within 30 days but has not paid after 45 days, the invoice may require a follow-up.

Contact the customer, confirm the payment status, and update your records after the payment is received.
05

Record Customer Receipts

When a customer pays an outstanding invoice, record the receipt in TallyPrime:

  1. Open the Receipt voucher.
  2. Select the appropriate bank or cash ledger.
  3. Select the customer ledger.
  4. Enter the amount received.
  5. Allocate the receipt against the correct outstanding bill reference, where applicable.
  6. Enter the payment details and save the voucher.

The receipt should be allocated correctly so that the relevant outstanding invoice is reduced or settled in the reports.

For a broader walkthrough, see How to Record Bank Transactions in TallyPrime.

06

Review the Updated Receivables Report

Return to the customer outstanding report and verify that the receipt has been reflected correctly. If the customer has paid the full amount, the invoice should normally no longer appear as outstanding. Partial payments may leave a remaining balance.

If the report does not reflect the expected result, review the receipt voucher, customer ledger, and bill allocation.

BANKING REPORTS

How to Track Payments Through Banking Reports in TallyPrime

Banking reports help accountants review transactions recorded against bank accounts and understand the movement of funds.

01

Open the Bank Book

Open the relevant bank ledger report or Bank Book in TallyPrime. The Bank Book shows transactions recorded against the selected bank account, such as receipts, supplier payments, transfers, and other bank-related entries.

Related guide: How to Check Bank Book in TallyPrime.

02

Select the Reporting Period

Choose the period you want to review, such as a specific week, month, or financial year. Reviewing transactions for October, for example, can help you identify customer receipts and supplier payments recorded during that month.

03

Review Bank Transactions

Check the transaction date, voucher type, narration, reference details, and amount. Look for customer receipts, supplier payments, bank transfers, bank charges, interest credited or debited, and transactions that may have been entered incorrectly.

The information available depends on how the company records its banking transactions.

04

Open the Relevant Voucher

If you need more information about a particular transaction, open the corresponding voucher or ledger entry. Verify the party name, amount, date, bank ledger, and accounting classification.

If an error is identified, correct the entry using the appropriate accounting process and retain supporting documentation.

05

Compare the Bank Book with the Bank Statement

Download or obtain the relevant bank statement and compare its transactions with those recorded in TallyPrime. Check whether deposits, withdrawals, bank charges, and other transactions appear in both records.

Any differences should be investigated rather than automatically treated as errors.
RECONCILIATION

How to Perform Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime

Bank reconciliation is the process of comparing transactions recorded in your books with those shown in your bank statement. It helps identify transactions that have not yet appeared in the bank account, bank charges that have not been recorded, incorrect entries, and other timing or recording differences.

Steps to Reconcile Bank Transactions

  1. Open the relevant bank ledger or Bank Reconciliation report.
  2. Select the period you want to reconcile.
  3. Review the transactions listed in TallyPrime.
  4. Compare each transaction with the bank statement.
  5. Identify transactions that match.
  6. Enter or verify the relevant bank dates and reconciliation details using the available options.
  7. Investigate missing or mismatched transactions.
  8. Review the reconciled and unreconciled balances.

The exact workflow may vary depending on your TallyPrime version, bank integration, and whether bank statements are imported.

Example: A one-day timing difference

A customer transfers ₹15,000 to your bank account on 10 October. The receipt is recorded in TallyPrime on 10 October, but the bank statement reflects the credit on 11 October. The difference may be a timing issue rather than an accounting error. Verify the transaction details and use the actual bank date as appropriate.

For a more detailed walkthrough, read How to Perform Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime and How to Match Bank Transactions With TallyPrime Entries.

PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

Practical Example: Tracking Receivables and Payments

Consider ABC Traders, which sells goods to three customers on credit.

Customer Invoice Amount Amount Received Outstanding
Customer A ₹25,000 ₹25,000 ₹0
Customer B ₹40,000 ₹15,000 ₹25,000
Customer C ₹30,000 ₹0 ₹30,000
Total ₹95,000 ₹40,000 ₹55,000

ABC Traders has recorded sales invoices totalling ₹95,000 and received ₹40,000 from customers. The remaining receivables are ₹55,000.

OUTSTANDING RECEIVABLES ₹55,000

Invoice total ₹95,000 − amount received ₹40,000 = outstanding customer balances of ₹55,000.

The accountant can use the receivables report to identify the outstanding invoices and the Bank Book to review the ₹40,000 in customer receipts recorded against the bank account. The accountant should then compare those receipts with the actual bank statement to confirm that the funds have been credited.

This process provides a clearer picture of the company's pending collections and recorded bank activity.

SUPPLIER MANAGEMENT

How to Track Supplier Payments in TallyPrime

In addition to customer receivables, businesses need to monitor amounts payable to suppliers.

01

Open the Payables Report

Navigate to the relevant outstanding bills or payables report. Review the supplier names, outstanding invoices, due dates, and amounts payable.

02

Identify Upcoming Payments

Check which supplier bills are due soon and which are overdue. This helps the business plan payments based on available cash and contractual payment terms.

03

Record Supplier Payments

When a payment is made, open the Payment voucher and select the appropriate bank ledger and supplier ledger. Enter the payment amount and allocate it against the correct outstanding bill reference, where applicable. Save the voucher and verify that the supplier's outstanding balance has been updated correctly.

04

Verify the Bank Transaction

Review the Bank Book and compare the payment with the bank statement. If the payment has not yet cleared, investigate whether the difference is due to timing or an incorrect entry.

TROUBLESHOOTING

Common Problems When Tracking Receivables and Payments

01

Customer Receipts Are Not Reducing Outstanding Balances

Possible reasons: The receipt was allocated to the wrong customer, was not allocated against the correct bill reference, or the voucher was recorded incorrectly.

Solution: Open the receipt voucher, verify the customer ledger and bill allocation, and correct the entry if necessary.
02

Bank Book and Bank Statement Do Not Match

Possible reasons: A transaction has not cleared, bank charges or interest have not been recorded, a transaction is missing or duplicated, or the recorded amount or date is incorrect.

Solution: Compare the records transaction by transaction, identify the cause of the difference, and record legitimate missing transactions.
03

Overdue Invoices Are Not Easy to Identify

Possible reasons: Due dates or credit periods are not configured correctly, outstanding balances have not been allocated properly, or the wrong reporting period has been selected.

Solution: Verify the invoice references, due-date configuration, and report filters.
04

Supplier Payments Remain Outstanding After Payment

Possible reasons: The payment voucher has not been recorded, the payment has not been allocated against the correct supplier bill, or the wrong supplier ledger was selected.

Solution: Verify the payment voucher, supplier ledger, and bill allocation.
05

Duplicate Receipts or Payments Appear

Possible reasons: A transaction was entered manually and imported again, the same payment was recorded in multiple vouchers, or an imported bank transaction was not matched to an existing entry.

Solution: Review the transaction history and supporting bank records before correcting duplicate entries.
ACCOUNTING CHECKLIST

Best Practices for Managing Receivables and Payments

To maintain accurate accounting records in TallyPrime, follow these practices:

  • Record sales invoices promptly.
  • Enter customer receipts and supplier payments accurately.
  • Allocate receipts and payments against the correct outstanding bills.
  • Review receivables and payables at regular intervals.
  • Follow up on overdue invoices.
  • Reconcile bank accounts regularly.
  • Investigate unreconciled transactions.
  • Maintain bank statements and payment confirmations.
  • Review the Day Book and relevant ledgers for unusual entries.
  • Back up company data according to your organisation's procedures.

These practices help improve cash-flow visibility and reduce avoidable accounting discrepancies.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How can I check customer outstanding payments in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant outstanding statements or receivables report and review the customer balances and bill references. The available details depend on the company's accounting configuration.

2. Which TallyPrime report is used to track bank transactions?

The Bank Book is commonly used to review transactions recorded against a bank ledger. The Bank Reconciliation report helps compare recorded transactions with the bank statement.

3. What is the difference between receivables and bank balance?

Receivables represent amounts customers owe the business. The bank balance represents the amount recorded in the bank account ledger, subject to reconciliation with the actual bank statement.

4. How do I record a customer payment in TallyPrime?

Use a Receipt voucher, select the appropriate customer and bank or cash ledger, enter the amount, and allocate the receipt against the correct outstanding bill where applicable.

5. How do I check overdue customer invoices in TallyPrime?

Review the receivables or outstanding bills report and use the available due-date or ageing options. Ensure the credit period and bill references are configured correctly.

6. Why is bank reconciliation important?

Bank reconciliation helps identify timing differences, missing transactions, bank charges, duplicate entries, and other discrepancies between the accounting records and bank statement.

7. Can TallyPrime help track supplier payments?

Yes. Payables or outstanding bills reports help monitor supplier balances, while Payment vouchers and the Bank Book help track recorded payments.

8. How often should a business reconcile its bank account?

The frequency depends on the volume of transactions and the business's internal controls. Many businesses reconcile bank accounts monthly, while businesses with frequent transactions may benefit from more frequent reviews.

FINAL TAKEAWAY

Conclusion

Tracking receivables and payments through banking and accounting reports in TallyPrime helps businesses monitor customer collections, manage supplier obligations, review bank transactions, and identify discrepancies.

The key is to use the appropriate report for each task: receivables reports for customer outstanding balances, payables reports for supplier obligations, the Bank Book for recorded bank activity, and Bank Reconciliation for comparing book entries with the bank statement.

For accounting students and aspiring accountants, practising these workflows builds valuable skills in accounts receivable, accounts payable, banking, and reconciliation. You can also use a Trial Balance report to review ledger balances as part of broader accounting checks.
