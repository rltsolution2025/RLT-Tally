01 / INTRODUCTION

Introduction

Understanding a business's financial performance is essential for making informed decisions. Business owners, accountants, and finance professionals need to know whether a company is profitable, can meet its short-term obligations, and is managing its assets and liabilities effectively.

This is where ratio analysis becomes useful.

Ratio analysis helps evaluate a company's financial position by comparing figures from financial statements such as the Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss Account. TallyPrime helps users maintain accounting records and access financial reports that provide the data needed for this analysis.

By learning how to use ratio analysis in TallyPrime, accounting students and professionals can develop practical skills in financial reporting, performance evaluation, and business decision-making.

In this guide, you will learn what ratio analysis is, how to access relevant reports in TallyPrime, the important financial ratios to understand, and how to interpret them using practical examples.

02 / BASIC CONCEPT

What Is Ratio Analysis?

Ratio analysis is a financial technique used to assess a business's performance by comparing two or more related financial figures.

These figures are generally obtained from the company's financial statements, including the Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss Account.

For example, the current ratio compares current assets with current liabilities. It helps assess whether a business has sufficient short-term assets to meet its short-term obligations.

Similarly, the net profit margin measures how much profit a business retains from its revenue after accounting for the relevant expenses.

Ratio analysis helps convert accounting figures into meaningful indicators that are easier to compare and interpret.

03 / BUSINESS VALUE

Why Is Ratio Analysis Important for Business Reporting?

Businesses use ratio analysis to:

Evaluate Performance

Evaluate profitability and financial performance.

Understand Liquidity

Understand short-term liquidity and payment capacity.

Assess Stability

Assess debt levels and long-term financial stability.

Monitor Efficiency

Monitor operating efficiency and resource use.

Compare Periods

Compare performance across accounting periods.

Support Planning

Identify trends and support budgeting and business planning.

Businesses also use ratios to present financial information to management and stakeholders. However, no single ratio can explain a company's entire financial position. Ratios should be interpreted together and compared with historical results, business objectives, and relevant industry benchmarks.

06 / FINANCIAL RATIO TYPES

Types of Financial Ratios Used in Business Reporting

Financial ratios are commonly divided into four main categories: liquidity, profitability, solvency, and efficiency ratios.

Liquidity RatiosShort-term payment capacity Profitability RatiosProfit generated from sales and operations Solvency RatiosLong-term financial stability Efficiency RatiosUse of inventory and working capital
06.1 / LIQUIDITY

1. Liquidity Ratios

Liquidity ratios measure a company's ability to meet its short-term financial obligations.

01
LIQUIDITY RATIO

Current Ratio

The current ratio compares current assets with current liabilities.

FORMULA
Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

Example

Suppose a business has current assets of ₹5,00,000 and current liabilities of ₹2,50,000.

₹5,00,000 ÷ ₹2,50,000 = 2:1
Current Ratio: 2:1

This means the business has ₹2 in current assets for every ₹1 in current liabilities.

A higher ratio may indicate stronger short-term coverage, but an excessively high ratio can also reflect inefficient use of cash or inventory. The appropriate level depends on the business and industry.

02
LIQUIDITY RATIO

Quick Ratio

The quick ratio measures the ability to meet short-term obligations using assets that can generally be converted into cash quickly.

COMMON FORMULA
Quick Ratio = (Current Assets − Inventory − Prepaid Expenses) ÷ Current Liabilities

Some businesses use a slightly different definition depending on their reporting policy.

Example

Current assets are ₹5,00,000, inventory is ₹1,50,000, prepaid expenses are ₹50,000, and current liabilities are ₹2,50,000.

(₹5,00,000 − ₹1,50,000 − ₹50,000) ÷ ₹2,50,000
₹3,00,000 ÷ ₹2,50,000 = 1.2:1
Quick Ratio: 1.2:1

This indicates ₹1.20 in quick assets for every ₹1 in current liabilities.

06.2 / PROFITABILITY

2. Profitability Ratios

Profitability ratios help assess how effectively a business generates profit from its revenue, assets, or capital.

03
PROFITABILITY RATIO

Gross Profit Ratio

The gross profit ratio measures gross profit as a percentage of net sales.

FORMULA
Gross Profit Ratio = (Gross Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100

Example

If net sales are ₹10,00,000 and gross profit is ₹3,00,000:

(₹3,00,000 ÷ ₹10,00,000) × 100 = 30%
Gross Profit Ratio: 30%

This means the business generates ₹30 in gross profit for every ₹100 of net sales.

A decline may be associated with rising purchase costs, pricing pressure, or changes in the sales mix. Further analysis is needed to identify the cause.

04
PROFITABILITY RATIO

Net Profit Ratio

The net profit ratio measures net profit as a percentage of net sales.

FORMULA
Net Profit Ratio = (Net Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100

Example

If net sales are ₹10,00,000 and net profit is ₹1,00,000:

(₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹10,00,000) × 100 = 10%
Net Profit Ratio: 10%

This means the business retains ₹10 in net profit for every ₹100 of net sales, based on the profit figure used in the calculation.

When comparing periods, use a consistent definition of net profit.

06.3 / SOLVENCY

3. Solvency Ratios

Solvency ratios help evaluate a company's long-term financial stability and dependence on debt.

05
SOLVENCY RATIO

Debt-to-Equity Ratio

The debt-to-equity ratio compares debt with shareholders' equity or owners' funds.

FORMULA
Debt-to-Equity Ratio = Total Debt ÷ Shareholders' Equity

Example

If a company has total debt of ₹4,00,000 and shareholders' equity of ₹8,00,000:

₹4,00,000 ÷ ₹8,00,000 = 0.5:1
Debt-to-Equity Ratio: 0.5:1

This indicates ₹0.50 in debt for every ₹1 of shareholders' equity.

The definition of debt should be consistent when comparing companies. Some analyses use total borrowings, while others use a broader measure of liabilities.

06
SOLVENCY RATIO

Interest Coverage Ratio

The interest coverage ratio measures how comfortably a business's operating earnings cover its interest expenses.

FORMULA
Interest Coverage Ratio = EBIT ÷ Interest Expense

EBIT means earnings before interest and tax.

Example

If EBIT is ₹3,00,000 and interest expense is ₹60,000:

₹3,00,000 ÷ ₹60,000 = 5 times
Interest Coverage Ratio: 5 times

This means the business generates operating earnings equal to five times its interest expense.

A lower ratio may indicate increased difficulty meeting interest obligations, especially if earnings are declining.

06.4 / EFFICIENCY

4. Efficiency Ratios

Efficiency ratios help assess how effectively a business manages its assets, inventory, and working capital.

07
EFFICIENCY RATIO

Inventory Turnover Ratio

The inventory turnover ratio measures how often inventory is sold or used during a period.

FORMULA
Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Inventory
Average Inventory = (Opening Inventory + Closing Inventory) ÷ 2

Example

If the cost of goods sold is ₹12,00,000 and average inventory is ₹2,00,000:

₹12,00,000 ÷ ₹2,00,000 = 6 times
Inventory Turnover Ratio: 6 times

This means average inventory turns over six times during the period.

A low ratio may indicate slow-moving inventory, while an unusually high ratio may indicate limited stock availability. Industry characteristics should be considered.

08
EFFICIENCY RATIO

Debtors Turnover Ratio

The debtors turnover ratio helps evaluate how efficiently a business collects amounts owed by customers.

COMMON FORMULA
Debtors Turnover Ratio = Net Credit Sales ÷ Average Trade Receivables

Example

If net credit sales are ₹15,00,000 and average trade receivables are ₹3,00,000:

₹15,00,000 ÷ ₹3,00,000 = 5 times
Debtors Turnover Ratio: 5 times

This suggests that average trade receivables turn over five times during the period.

A lower ratio may indicate slower collections, but credit terms and industry practices must also be considered.

08 / BUSINESS APPLICATION

How to Use Ratio Analysis for Better Business Decisions

Ratio analysis becomes more valuable when businesses use it to investigate performance and take action.

1. Improve Cash Flow Management

Review liquidity ratios to identify potential pressure on short-term obligations. If liquidity is declining, examine receivables, inventory levels, supplier payment terms, and cash balances.

2. Monitor Profitability

Compare gross and net profit ratios across accounting periods. Investigate purchase costs, selling prices, discounts, product mix, operating expenses, and other costs when ratios change.

3. Control Inventory

Use inventory turnover information to identify products that move slowly or require excessive working capital. Review stock reports and purchase patterns before changing stock levels.

4. Manage Customer Collections

Review debtor balances and collection trends to identify overdue receivables. Businesses may use this information to improve follow-up procedures and credit policies.

5. Evaluate Borrowings

Use debt-related ratios and interest coverage to understand borrowing levels and the business's capacity to service debt. Consider cash flow forecasts and loan repayment schedules alongside these ratios.

6. Prepare Management Reports

Combine ratio analysis with the Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss Account, cash flow information, and other relevant reports. Explain what changed, possible reasons, and actions being considered.

You can build a broader view by reviewing the Cash Flow report, Bank Reconciliation, and receivables and payables alongside your financial ratios.

09 / ACCOUNTING ACCURACY

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using Ratio Analysis in TallyPrime

1. Using Incomplete Accounting Data

Missing sales entries, purchase transactions, expenses, or adjustments can distort reported ratios.

Solution: Complete and review the accounting records before analysing the results.

2. Incorrect Ledger Classification

Misclassified assets, liabilities, income, or expenses can affect financial statements and ratio calculations.

Solution: Review ledger groups and classifications regularly.

3. Comparing Different Reporting Periods

Comparing a full financial year with only a few months can produce misleading conclusions.

Solution: Use comparable periods and account for seasonality where relevant.

4. Relying on Only One Ratio

A business can have strong profitability but weak liquidity, or low debt but poor operating performance.

Solution: Evaluate multiple ratios together.

5. Treating Every Ratio as a Universal Benchmark

A suitable ratio for a retail business may differ from one for a manufacturing company or a service provider.

Solution: Compare results with historical performance and relevant industry benchmarks.

6. Ignoring Non-Financial Factors

Financial ratios do not fully explain customer satisfaction, product quality, staff productivity, or market changes.

Solution: Combine financial analysis with operational information and business context.

10 / FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is ratio analysis in TallyPrime?

Ratio analysis in TallyPrime involves evaluating financial indicators using accounting information from reports such as the Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss Account. It helps users understand liquidity, profitability, solvency, and operational efficiency.

2. How do I open the Ratio Analysis report in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant company, navigate to the financial reporting area, and look for Ratio Analysis among the available reports. The exact menu path may vary by TallyPrime release.

3. Which financial ratios are useful for business reporting?

Commonly used ratios include the current ratio, quick ratio, gross profit ratio, net profit ratio, debt-to-equity ratio, interest coverage ratio, inventory turnover ratio, and debtors turnover ratio.

4. Can TallyPrime calculate financial ratios automatically?

Depending on the TallyPrime version and report configuration, financial ratios may be displayed automatically in the relevant report. Some specialised ratios may require additional calculations using figures from accounting reports.

5. Why is ratio analysis important for accountants?

Ratio analysis helps accountants interpret financial statements, identify trends, investigate changes in performance, and provide useful information for management decisions.

6. What is the difference between liquidity and profitability ratios?

Liquidity ratios assess the ability to meet short-term obligations. Profitability ratios measure how effectively a business generates profit from sales, assets, or capital.

7. Can ratio analysis help small businesses?

Yes. Small businesses can use ratio analysis to monitor cash flow, profitability, inventory, receivables, and borrowing levels. Regular reviews can help identify potential issues before they become more serious.

8. Is ratio analysis enough to determine whether a business is financially healthy?

No. Ratio analysis is one part of financial evaluation. It should be combined with cash flow analysis, financial statements, business conditions, industry comparisons, and other relevant information.
