Introduction

Recording business transactions accurately is one of the most important responsibilities of an accountant. Whether a business receives money from a customer, pays office rent, purchases goods, sells products or transfers money between bank accounts, every transaction must be recorded using the appropriate accounting method.

In TallyPrime, vouchers are used to record and manage business transactions. Different voucher types are available for accounting entries, sales, purchases, banking, inventory movements, orders and other business activities.

This guide explains major TallyPrime voucher types with practical examples, commonly used shortcut keys and situations in which each voucher is used.

What Is a Voucher in TallyPrime?

A voucher in TallyPrime is a record used to enter and maintain details of a business transaction. It can capture the transaction date, ledger accounts, amount, inventory details where applicable and narration.

Example

If a business pays ₹10,000 as office rent through its bank account, the accountant can record it using a Payment Voucher. A customer payment can be recorded using a Receipt Voucher, while goods sold to a customer are generally recorded using a Sales Voucher.

In simple terms: a voucher records a business transaction in the appropriate format so accounting records and relevant reports can be updated.

How Many Types of Vouchers Are There in TallyPrime?

The number of voucher types available can depend on enabled features, configuration and TallyPrime version. The main categories include:

01

Accounting vouchers

Payments, receipts, sales, purchases and adjustments.

02

Order vouchers

Customer orders and orders placed with suppliers.

03

Inventory vouchers

Goods received, dispatched, transferred or counted.

04

Payroll & attendance

Payroll and attendance information when configured.

05

Special-purpose vouchers

Temporary records and reversing adjustments.

CATEGORY 1

Accounting Voucher Types in TallyPrime

Accounting vouchers record financial transactions such as payments, receipts, sales, purchases and adjustments.

01

Contra Voucher

Common shortcut: F4

Used for movement of funds between cash and bank accounts or between bank accounts belonging to the same business.

Example: Depositing ₹20,000 cash into a bank

Account Debit Credit
Bank Account ₹20,000 —
Cash Account — ₹20,000

Use it for: cash deposits, cash withdrawals from bank and transfers between company bank accounts.

Paying office rent through the bank is generally a Payment Voucher transaction, not a Contra Voucher.

02

Payment Voucher

Common shortcut: F5

Records money paid by the business through cash, bank transfer, cheque or another payment method.

Example: Paying ₹15,000 office rent through bank

Account Debit Credit
Office Rent ₹15,000 —
Bank Account — ₹15,000

Use it for: rent, salaries, electricity bills, supplier payments and office expenses.

The entry may vary when tax deductions, outstanding liabilities or other adjustments apply.

03

Receipt Voucher

Common shortcut: F6

Records money received by the business, such as customer payments, capital contributions or other receipts.

Example: ABC Traders pays ₹25,000 against an outstanding invoice

Account Debit Credit
Bank Account ₹25,000 —
ABC Traders — ₹25,000

Use it for: customer receipts, interest income, capital contributions and other money received.

Allocate the receipt against the relevant outstanding bill when bill-wise tracking is enabled and applicable.

04

Journal Voucher

Common shortcut: F7

Records accounting adjustments that do not fit more appropriately into a specialized voucher type. Common uses include depreciation, accruals and provisions.

Example: Recording ₹5,000 depreciation

Account Debit Credit
Depreciation Expense ₹5,000 —
Accumulated Depreciation — ₹5,000

Use it for: depreciation, accrued expenses, provisions and suitable adjustments.

Use a specialized voucher when one is available and appropriate; do not use Journal for every transaction.

05

Sales Voucher

Common shortcut: F8

Records sales of goods or services, including cash sales, credit sales and applicable GST transactions.

Example: Credit sale of ₹10,000 plus illustrative 18% intra-state GST

Account Debit Credit
Customer ₹11,800 —
Sales Account — ₹10,000
Output CGST — ₹900
Output SGST — ₹900

Use it for: product or service sales, sales invoices and applicable GST.

Tax treatment depends on supply type, place of supply, registration status and applicable GST rules.

06

Purchase Voucher

Common shortcut: F9

Records goods or services purchased by the business, including cash purchases, credit purchases and applicable GST.

Example: Credit purchase of ₹8,000 plus illustrative 18% intra-state GST

Account Debit Credit
Purchase Account ₹8,000 —
Input CGST ₹720 —
Input SGST ₹720 —
Supplier — ₹9,440

Use it for: goods for resale, raw materials, business purchases and applicable GST.

Input tax credit should be recorded and claimed only when the legal conditions are satisfied.

07

Credit Note Voucher

Common shortcut: Alt+F6*

Commonly records a reduction in the amount receivable from a customer, such as sales returns or eligible price reductions.

Example: Customer returns ₹2,000 of goods plus illustrative 18% GST

Account Debit Credit
Sales Returns ₹2,000 —
Output CGST ₹180 —
Output SGST ₹180 —
Customer — ₹2,360

Use it for: sales returns and qualifying reductions in receivables.

*Shortcut availability depends on voucher-entry context/configuration. Tax adjustments must follow GST rules.

08

Debit Note Voucher

Common shortcut: Alt+F5*

Commonly records purchase returns or qualifying adjustments that reduce the amount payable to a supplier.

Example: Return ₹1,000 of goods plus illustrative 18% GST

Account Debit Credit
Supplier ₹1,180 —
Purchase Returns — ₹1,000
Input CGST — ₹90
Input SGST — ₹90

Use it for: purchase returns and qualifying supplier-balance adjustments.

*Shortcut availability can vary. Treatment depends on the original transaction, ITC claimed and tax rules.

CATEGORY 2

Order Voucher Types in TallyPrime

Order vouchers track orders before or during invoicing. An order does not necessarily mean that goods have been delivered or a sale completed.

09

Sales Order Voucher

Customer order

Records an order received from a customer. Example: ABC Traders orders 100 units at ₹500 each, totalling ₹50,000 before taxes. Use it to track pending quantities and fulfilment before completing the sale.

10

Purchase Order Voucher

Supplier order

Records an order placed with a supplier. Example: Your business orders 200 units at ₹150 each, totalling ₹30,000 before taxes. Use it to monitor the goods ordered and compare them with quantities received.

CATEGORY 3

Inventory Voucher Types in TallyPrime

Inventory vouchers help record the receipt, delivery, movement and adjustment of stock.

11

Receipt Note Voucher

Goods received

Records goods received from a supplier, generally before or separately from the purchase invoice. Example: If 100 units were ordered but only 80 arrive, record the received quantity and track the remaining 20.

12

Delivery Note Voucher

Goods dispatched

Records delivery or dispatch of goods to a customer. Example: If a customer orders 50 units and 30 are dispatched first, record the 30 units delivered.

13

Stock Journal Voucher

Common shortcut: Alt+F7

Records stock transfers, consumption, production-related movements and supported inventory adjustments. Example: Transfer 20 units from Warehouse A to Warehouse B.

14

Physical Stock Voucher

Common shortcut: Ctrl+F7*

Records physically counted stock to reconcile actual quantities with stock records. Example: If the system shows 100 units but a physical count finds 96, record the verified quantity and investigate the difference.

15

Rejections In Voucher

Customer rejection

Records goods rejected by a customer and returned to the business in an inventory workflow. Example: Record five damaged products returned by a customer.

16

Rejections Out Voucher

Supplier rejection

Records goods rejected by the business and returned to a supplier. Example: Record 10 defective units returned to the supplier.

Inventory voucher names and workflows depend on enabled inventory features and TallyPrime configuration.

CATEGORY 4

Payroll and Attendance Voucher Types

17

Payroll Voucher

Payroll workflow

Records payroll transactions when payroll features are enabled. Example: A business processes monthly salaries, including applicable salary expenses, deductions and payable amounts.

18

Attendance Voucher

Attendance details

Records employee attendance or related information when configured. Example: Record attendance and paid leave for a payroll period.

CATEGORY 5

Special-Purpose Voucher Types

19

Memorandum Voucher

Temporary record

Records a provisional transaction without immediately affecting regular books like an ordinary posted voucher. Example: Record a provisional expense while waiting for final confirmation, then regularize it later.

20

Reversing Journal Voucher

Temporary adjustment

Supports accounting adjustments intended to reverse on a specified date or through a configured workflow. Example: Record a period-end adjustment for reporting and reverse it in the next period.

Specialized vouchers may need to be enabled. Confirm availability in your installed TallyPrime release.

How to Select the Correct Voucher in TallyPrime

Choose the voucher based on what actually happened in the business.

Money paidPayment Voucher
Money receivedReceipt Voucher
Cash-bank transferContra Voucher
Goods or services soldSales Voucher
Goods or services purchasedPurchase Voucher
Accounting adjustmentJournal Voucher, where appropriate
Customer orderSales Order Voucher
Supplier orderPurchase Order Voucher
Internal stock transferStock Journal Voucher
Physical stock countPhysical Stock Voucher
Before saving a voucher:

Verify the date, ledger accounts, amounts, inventory quantities, applicable taxes and supporting documents. For returned or rejected goods, choose the workflow that matches the original transaction.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using TallyPrime Vouchers

01

Using the wrong voucher type

Paying office rent should generally use a Payment Voucher rather than a Contra Voucher.

02

Confusing orders with invoices

A Sales Order records a customer order. A Sales Voucher records the actual sales transaction.

03

Recording incorrect GST details

GST treatment depends on the transaction, applicable rules and input tax credit eligibility.

04

Ignoring inventory quantities

Check quantities, units and inventory ledgers when maintaining stock.

05

Saving without checking details

Verify ledger accounts, amount, date, narration and tax information before accepting a voucher.

06

Using Journal Vouchers for everything

Use specialized vouchers when they accurately represent the transaction and support the workflow.

How to Practise Voucher Entries in TallyPrime

Practise these transactions in a sample company:

  1. Deposit cash into the bank using a Contra Voucher.
  2. Pay office rent using a Payment Voucher.
  3. Receive money from a customer using a Receipt Voucher.
  4. Record depreciation using a Journal Voucher.
  5. Record a cash or credit sale using a Sales Voucher.
  6. Record a supplier purchase using a Purchase Voucher.
  7. Record an eligible sales return using a Credit Note.
  8. Record an eligible purchase return using a Debit Note.
  9. Create a Sales Order and record delivery and invoice using the appropriate workflow.
  10. Transfer stock between warehouses using a Stock Journal.
  11. Record a physical stock count.
  12. Review resulting ledgers and financial reports.

Practice helps you understand how voucher types connect to accounting concepts and real business operations.

Frequently Asked Questions About TallyPrime Voucher Types

1. What are the main voucher types in TallyPrime?

The main accounting types include Contra, Payment, Receipt, Journal, Sales, Purchase, Debit Note and Credit Note. TallyPrime also supports order, inventory, payroll and special-purpose vouchers.

2. What is the shortcut key for Payment Voucher?

F5 is the commonly used shortcut for Payment Voucher in the standard voucher-entry workflow.

3. What is the shortcut key for Receipt Voucher?

F6 is the commonly used shortcut for Receipt Voucher.

4. What is the shortcut key for Sales Voucher?

F8 is the commonly used shortcut for Sales Voucher.

5. What is the shortcut key for Purchase Voucher?

F9 is the commonly used shortcut for Purchase Voucher.

6. What is the difference between Payment and Contra Vouchers?

A Payment Voucher records money paid for expenses, suppliers and other payments. A Contra Voucher records fund movements between cash and bank accounts or between the business's bank accounts.

7. What is the difference between Sales and Sales Order Vouchers?

A Sales Order records a customer order. A Sales Voucher records the actual sales transaction. An order does not automatically mean goods have been delivered or invoiced.

8. What is the difference between Debit Note and Credit Note?

A Credit Note commonly records qualifying reductions in amounts receivable from customers. A Debit Note commonly records qualifying purchase returns or supplier adjustments. Treatment depends on the original transaction and applicable rules.

9. Which voucher transfers stock between warehouses?

A Stock Journal Voucher is commonly used for internal stock transfers when the inventory workflow is configured.

10. Can beginners learn TallyPrime voucher entries?

Yes. Start with accounting fundamentals, then practise payments, receipts, sales, purchases and journal adjustments.

Conclusion

Understanding TallyPrime voucher types is an essential step toward learning computerized accounting. Each voucher has a specific purpose, from daily payments and receipts to sales, purchases, inventory movements, payroll and accounting adjustments.

Practise realistic business transactions and review ledger balances and financial reports. This helps you understand not only which voucher to select but why a transaction is recorded that way.
