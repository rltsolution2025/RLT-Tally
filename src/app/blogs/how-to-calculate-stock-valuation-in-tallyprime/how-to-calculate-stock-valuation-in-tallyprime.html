02 / BASICS

What Is Stock Valuation?

Stock valuation is the process of determining the monetary value of goods held by a business at a particular point in time.

It includes calculating the value of inventory remaining after purchases, sales, and other stock movements have been recorded.

BASIC STOCK VALUATION FORMULA
Stock Value = Quantity × Cost per Unit

For example, if a business has 100 units of a product and the applicable valuation cost is ₹200 per unit:

100 × ₹200 = ₹20,000

The actual valuation depends on the stock valuation method selected, the purchase costs, stock movements, and the applicable accounting policy.

Stock valuation is not always the same as multiplying the current quantity by the latest purchase rate. The selected valuation method and transaction history determine the closing value.

Before configuring valuation methods, make sure the underlying inventory masters are set up correctly. See our guide on creating stock items in TallyPrime.

04 / VALUATION METHODS

What Are the Stock Valuation Methods in TallyPrime?

TallyPrime supports multiple stock valuation methods. The available methods and their suitability depend on the stock item, configuration, and accounting requirements.

01
VALUATION METHOD

FIFO (First In, First Out)

Under FIFO, the oldest inventory costs are generally assigned to goods sold first, while the costs of more recently purchased inventory remain in closing stock.

Example

Purchase Quantity Cost per Unit
First purchase 10 units ₹100
Second purchase 10 units ₹120

If 10 units are sold, FIFO assigns the first purchase cost of ₹100 per unit to those units, assuming no other stock movements or valuation adjustments.

Remaining stock value10 × ₹120 = ₹1,200

FIFO is commonly used when inventory costs need to reflect the sequence of purchase layers.

02
VALUATION METHOD

Average Cost Method

Under an average cost method, inventory is valued using an average of the relevant costs.

WEIGHTED-AVERAGE FORMULA
Weighted Average Cost per Unit = Total Cost of Goods Available ÷ Total Units Available

Example

Purchase Quantity Cost per Unit Total Cost
First purchase 10 ₹100 ₹1,000
Second purchase 10 ₹120 ₹1,200
Total 20 — ₹2,200

Weighted-average cost per unit: ₹2,200 ÷ 20 = ₹110.

If 10 units remain, their value would be:

Remaining stock value10 × ₹110 = ₹1,100

This example illustrates a weighted average across the two purchases. The exact result in TallyPrime depends on the selected valuation method and the timing of inventory transactions.

03
VALUATION METHOD

LIFO (Last In, First Out)

LIFO assigns the most recent inventory costs to goods sold first.

Using the earlier example, if 10 units are sold, the latest purchase cost of ₹120 per unit would be assigned to those units under LIFO. The remaining 10 units would be valued at ₹100 each.

Remaining stock value10 × ₹100 = ₹1,000

Accounting note: LIFO is not permitted under IFRS and is generally not accepted under Indian accounting standards applicable to inventory valuation. Do not select a method simply because it appears in a theoretical comparison; use a valuation method permitted by the applicable accounting framework.

04
VALUATION METHOD

Standard Cost

Under standard-cost valuation, inventory is valued using a predetermined standard cost.

For example, if the standard cost is ₹150 per unit and 100 units remain in stock, the standard valuation would be:

Illustrative standard valuation100 × ₹150 = ₹15,000

Standard cost requires a suitable costing system and review of differences between standard and actual costs.

05
VALUATION METHOD

Actual Purchase Cost or Other Supported Methods

Businesses may use other supported methods or configurations to value inventory according to their accounting requirements.

The appropriate method depends on the type of inventory, the applicable accounting framework, and the features available in the TallyPrime version being used.

05 / TALLYPRIME SETUP

How to Configure Stock Valuation in TallyPrime

Before calculating stock valuation, make sure the company has been configured to maintain inventory. Follow these steps.

01

Open Your Company

  1. Open TallyPrime.
  2. Select the company whose inventory you want to manage.
  3. Confirm that inventory features are enabled.
  4. Verify that the relevant stock groups, units, and stock items are available.

The exact menu names may vary by TallyPrime release.

02

Create or Open a Stock Item

  1. Navigate to the inventory or stock item creation screen.
  2. Select the option to create a stock item, or open an existing item.
  3. Enter the stock item name and select the appropriate stock group.
  4. Specify the unit of measurement, such as pieces, kilograms, or litres.
  5. Enter any other required stock details.

For example, create a stock item called Office Chair and select Nos as its unit.

For detailed setup instructions, read How to Create Stock Items in TallyPrime.

03

Select the Valuation Method

In the stock item configuration, locate the applicable costing or valuation method field. Depending on the version and setup, you may be able to select a supported method such as FIFO, average cost, or another appropriate method.

Choose the method that matches your accounting policy and inventory requirements.

If you are learning TallyPrime, use a test company to compare valuation results before changing the method in a live company. Changing valuation settings can affect inventory values and reported financial figures.

04

Enter Opening Stock, If Applicable

If the business already has inventory when its records are created, enter the correct opening quantity and value where required.

How to Calculate Stock Valuation in TallyPrime: Practical Example

Let us consider a business that buys and sells office chairs. Assume that all transactions relate to the same stock item, with no opening stock, returns, additional inventory costs, or other adjustments.

Inventory Transactions

Transaction Quantity Rate Amount
Purchase 1 10 units ₹2,000 ₹20,000
Purchase 2 10 units ₹2,200 ₹22,000
Sale 5 units — —

Remaining purchase layer Calculation Value

Weighted-average cost per unit

₹42,000 ÷ 20 units₹2,100 per unit

Drill Down into Individual Items

Reconcile Inventory Records

08 / TROUBLESHOOTING

Common Stock Valuation Problems in TallyPrime

1. Closing Stock Value Is Incorrect

Possible causes: Incorrect purchase rates, missing purchase or sales vouchers, incorrect opening stock values, wrong valuation method, or unrecorded stock adjustments.

Solution: Review the stock item configuration and underlying transactions. Compare the reported quantity and value with supporting inventory records.

2. Stock Quantity Does Not Match Physical Inventory

Possible causes: Unrecorded purchases or sales, incorrect quantities, missing transfers or adjustments, or incorrect units of measurement.

Solution: Conduct a stock reconciliation and record valid corrections through the appropriate inventory workflow.

3. Average Cost Is Different from the Expected Amount

Possible causes: The selected valuation method differs from the manual calculation, purchase costs or transaction dates differ, or other inventory movements affect the valuation.

Solution: Confirm the selected valuation method and calculate the value using the same transactions and assumptions as the software.

4. Stock Value Changes After Modifying the Valuation Method

Possible cause: Different valuation methods can assign different costs to the inventory sold and the inventory remaining.

Solution: Review the accounting implications before changing the method in a live company. Follow the applicable accounting policy and obtain professional advice if the change affects reported financial statements.

09 / ACCOUNTING PRACTICES

Best Practices for Accurate Stock Valuation

To maintain reliable inventory values in TallyPrime:

  • Choose a valuation method appropriate to the business and applicable accounting standards.
  • Record purchases and sales promptly.
  • Maintain accurate stock item descriptions and units.
  • Verify opening stock values.
  • Review inventory reports regularly.
  • Reconcile physical stock with accounting records.
  • Investigate negative stock and unexpected valuation changes.
  • Maintain supporting purchase and sales documents.
  • Review the treatment of freight and other costs where relevant.
  • Avoid changing valuation settings without understanding the accounting impact.

Following these practices helps improve inventory accuracy and supports reliable financial reporting.

10 / FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I calculate stock valuation in TallyPrime?

Configure the stock item and its appropriate valuation method, record opening stock and inventory transactions, and review the relevant inventory reports. The closing stock value depends on the quantities, costs, and selected valuation method.

2. Which stock valuation methods are available in TallyPrime?

TallyPrime supports multiple inventory valuation methods. Availability depends on the version and configuration. Check the valuation options in your installation and select a method suitable for the applicable accounting requirements.

3. What is FIFO in TallyPrime?

FIFO stands for First In, First Out. It generally assigns the oldest inventory costs to goods sold first, leaving more recent purchase costs in closing inventory.

4. What is the weighted-average stock valuation method?

Weighted-average valuation calculates an average cost using the relevant quantities and costs. Closing stock is then valued using that average cost, subject to the selected method's calculation rules.

5. How can I check closing stock value in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant inventory reports, such as Stock Summary, and select the required reporting date. Review the closing quantities and values, then drill down into stock item details if necessary.

6. Why is stock valuation important in accounting?

Stock valuation helps determine closing inventory and the cost of goods sold. These values affect the calculation of profit and the presentation of inventory in financial statements.

7. Does stock valuation affect profit?

Yes. Under the applicable inventory accounting rules, the valuation of closing stock affects the cost of goods sold and reported profit. Accurate valuation is therefore important for reliable financial statements.

8. Can I change the stock valuation method in TallyPrime?

The available configuration options depend on the software version and stock item setup. Before changing the method in a live company, assess the effect on inventory values, accounting records, and financial reporting.
