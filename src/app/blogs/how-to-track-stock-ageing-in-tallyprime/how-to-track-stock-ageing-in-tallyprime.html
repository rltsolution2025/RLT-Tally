INTRODUCTION

How to Track Stock Ageing in TallyPrime

Managing inventory efficiently is essential for businesses that buy, sell, or store goods. Products that remain in a warehouse for too long can block working capital, increase storage costs, and create a risk of damage, expiry, or obsolescence.

Stock ageing helps businesses understand how long inventory has remained unsold or in stock and identify products that may require attention. TallyPrime provides inventory reports that help businesses review stock quantities, values, and movement. Depending on the report and features available in your version, you can use inventory information to assess ageing and identify slow-moving stock.

What will you learn?

You will learn what stock ageing means, how to review stock information in TallyPrime, how to analyse ageing using a practical example, and how to improve inventory management.

BASIC CONCEPT

What Is Stock Ageing?

Stock ageing is the process of analysing inventory according to how long it has remained in stock or how much time has passed since it was received or purchased.

A business may classify its inventory into the following age groups:

0–30 days
Recently received stock
31–60 days
Stock held for several weeks
61–90 days
Inventory for closer review
More than 90 days
Older inventory requiring review

These categories help businesses identify recently received stock, inventory that has remained unsold for several weeks, and products that may be moving too slowly.

Stock ageing is particularly useful for retailers, wholesalers, distributors, manufacturers, and businesses that manage large quantities of inventory.

BUSINESS BENEFITS

Why Is Stock Ageing Important?

Tracking stock ageing helps businesses:

Identify slow-moving and non-moving inventory.
Reduce unnecessary storage costs.
Avoid potential expiry or obsolescence losses.
Improve purchasing and replenishment decisions.
Manage working capital more effectively.
Plan discounts or promotional offers for ageing products.
Improve inventory control and reporting.

For accounting professionals, stock ageing analysis also helps connect inventory management with stock valuation, purchase planning, and business profitability.

REPORT OVERVIEW

What Is a Stock Ageing Report?

A stock ageing report groups inventory according to its age, usually based on a defined date or period. Depending on the software and reporting method, stock age may be measured from the purchase date, goods-receipt date, batch date, or another relevant inventory date.

A typical stock ageing report may include:

Particulars Meaning
Stock item Name of the product
Quantity available Current quantity in stock
Stock value Value of the inventory
Ageing period Number of days or period for which stock has been held
Ageing category Group such as 0–30 or 31–60 days
Slow-moving status Indicates inventory that may require review

Important: Current stock quantity alone does not reveal the age of each item. To calculate accurate stock ageing, the business needs suitable historical purchase, receipt, batch, or inventory movement information.

STEP-BY-STEP TUTORIAL

How to Track Stock Ageing in TallyPrime

The exact navigation and availability of ageing-specific reports depend on your TallyPrime release, configuration, and inventory tracking method. Use the following workflow to review stock movement and prepare an ageing analysis.

01
STEP 1

Enable Inventory Features

First, verify that inventory management is configured for your company.

  1. Open TallyPrime.
  2. Select the relevant company.
  3. Review the company features and inventory settings.
  4. Ensure that inventory features are enabled.
  5. Verify that stock items, units of measurement, and stock groups are configured correctly.
  6. Save any required changes.

If your business tracks batches, expiry dates, or additional inventory details, verify that the corresponding features are configured appropriately. Learn more in How to Manage Batch-Wise Inventory in TallyPrime.

02
STEP 2

Create Stock Items Correctly

Accurate stock item records are important for reliable inventory reporting. For each stock item, maintain relevant information such as:

  • Item name and description.
  • Stock group.
  • Unit of measurement.
  • Opening balance, where applicable.
  • Purchase and sales details.
  • Batch or expiry details, where relevant.

Consistent item names and classifications make it easier to review inventory across different reports. See the guide on How to Create Stock Items in TallyPrime.

03
STEP 3

Record Purchase and Stock Receipt Transactions

Stock ageing depends on reliable historical information. Record purchase transactions and inventory receipts with the correct dates and item details.

If goods are received on a different date from the supplier invoice, maintain the appropriate supporting records and use a consistent ageing policy. Where applicable, use batch details or other supported tracking information to distinguish inventory received at different times.

For more information about transaction entry, see TallyPrime Voucher Types Explained.

04
STEP 4

Review Stock Summary

Open the inventory reports in TallyPrime and review the Stock Summary report. It helps you understand the available inventory position, including quantities and values, depending on the report configuration.

Use this report to identify:

  • Items with high stock quantities.
  • Products with large inventory values.
  • Stock groups holding significant working capital.
  • Items that may need further ageing analysis.

Note: Stock Summary is an inventory-position report; it should not automatically be treated as a report showing the age of every remaining stock unit. Read How to Check Stock Summary in TallyPrime.

05
STEP 5

Review Stock Movement and Related Reports

Review the inventory reports available in your TallyPrime installation, such as stock item details, stock movement, purchase records, and sales records.

Use the available period and item filters to understand when inventory was purchased, received, sold, or otherwise moved. This helps establish the historical information required for ageing analysis.

If your installation includes a suitable ageing-specific report or add-on, use the report's documented calculation method and verify the results against your inventory records. You can also refer to How to Track Stock Movement in TallyPrime.

06
STEP 6

Calculate Stock Ageing Using Historical Records

If your available TallyPrime reports do not directly provide the ageing buckets you need, you can prepare an ageing analysis from exported inventory records and historical transactions.

Consider a business reviewing stock as of 30 September 2026.

Stock Item Receipt Date Quantity Remaining Age as of 30 Sep 2026
Product A 15 Sep 2026 20 15 days
Product B 10 Aug 2026 15 51 days
Product C 1 Jul 2026 10 91 days

The age is calculated as the number of elapsed calendar days between the relevant receipt date and the report date.

Ageing categories in this example

Product A: 0–30 days Product B: 31–60 days Product C: 91 days or more

These calculations assume the remaining quantities can be associated with the stated receipt dates. If the same item was received in multiple batches, use the appropriate batch or inventory allocation method rather than assigning the entire current balance to a single purchase date.

07
STEP 7

Identify Slow-Moving Stock

Once the inventory is grouped by age, review items that have remained in stock beyond your preferred period. For example, a business may flag items that have been held for more than 90 days for further review.

However, ageing alone does not necessarily mean an item is slow-moving. Some products are intentionally held for long periods or have seasonal demand. Compare stock age with sales history, demand forecasts, expiry dates, and purchasing plans before making decisions.

08
STEP 8

Review and Update Inventory Decisions

Use the ageing analysis to make practical inventory decisions. You may decide to:

  • Reduce or pause purchases of overstocked items.
  • Promote older but saleable products.
  • Transfer stock between locations, where appropriate.
  • Review expired, damaged, or obsolete goods.
  • Improve reorder levels.
  • Reconcile differences between physical stock and accounting records.

Review the results regularly so that inventory decisions are based on current and reliable information.

Practical Example: Stock Ageing Analysis

Suppose ABC Traders has the following inventory as of 30 September 2026.

Product Quantity Value per Unit Total Stock Value Age
Product A 20 ₹500 ₹10,000 15 days
Product B 15 ₹800 ₹12,000 51 days
Product C 10 ₹1,000 ₹10,000 91 days
Total 45 — ₹32,000 —

The business can classify its inventory as follows:

Ageing Category Stock Value
0–30 days ₹10,000
31–60 days ₹12,000
61–90 days ₹0
More than 90 days ₹10,000
Total ₹32,000

What should ABC Traders review?

₹10,000 of stock falls into the more-than-90-days category. The business should investigate whether Product C is still selling regularly, whether demand has declined, and whether the stock has expiry or obsolescence risks.

This example is illustrative. In a real inventory system, stock value should be based on the company's applicable valuation method, and the ageing calculation should follow the actual inventory receipt and movement records.

INVENTORY ANALYSIS

How to Identify Slow-Moving and Non-Moving Stock

Stock ageing and stock movement analysis work best when used together.

Slow-Moving Stock

Products that sell less frequently than expected or remain in inventory longer than the business's target period. An item purchased regularly but sold only once in several months may need a review of its reorder quantity.

Non-Moving Stock

Inventory that has not recorded movement for a defined period, according to the business's chosen measurement method. Check demand, planned projects, and seasonal patterns before treating it as obsolete.

Ageing vs. Stock Movement

Stock Ageing Stock Movement
Measures how long stock has been held Measures purchases, sales, receipts, and issues
Helps identify older inventory Helps identify sales and usage patterns
Supports expiry and obsolescence reviews Supports demand and replenishment planning
Requires reliable inventory age information Requires accurate transaction records

Using both analyses provides a better understanding of inventory performance than relying on either one alone. See How to Track Stock Movement in TallyPrime for a related tutorial.

TROUBLESHOOTING

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Tracking Stock Ageing

1. Using the Purchase Invoice Date for Every Situation

The invoice date may differ from the actual goods-receipt date. Decide which date is appropriate for your business's ageing policy and apply it consistently.

2. Ignoring Multiple Receipts of the Same Item

If an item was purchased on different dates, its remaining quantity may include stock of different ages. A single item-level balance may not be enough to calculate accurate ageing.

3. Confusing Stock Ageing with Expiry Tracking

Stock ageing measures time held in inventory. Expiry tracking identifies products that are approaching or have passed an expiry date. These are related but different analyses.

4. Relying Only on Current Stock Quantity

A stock balance tells you how much inventory is available, not necessarily how old it is. Use historical records and appropriate inventory allocation methods.

5. Ignoring Stock Valuation

Ageing analysis should be interpreted alongside the company's inventory valuation method and accounting records. Do not assume that age alone determines the correct accounting value.

6. Not Reviewing Reports Regularly

Infrequent reporting can allow old inventory to accumulate unnoticed. Set a review schedule that suits your business, such as weekly for fast-moving goods or monthly for general inventory.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is stock ageing in TallyPrime?

Stock ageing is the process of analysing how long inventory has remained in stock. TallyPrime's inventory records and reports can support this analysis, but the availability of a dedicated ageing report depends on the version, configuration, and tracking method.

2. How do I check stock ageing in TallyPrime?

Start with the Stock Summary and relevant stock item, purchase, and stock movement reports. If your version provides an ageing-specific report, verify its calculation method. Otherwise, use historical transaction records to prepare ageing buckets.

3. Does TallyPrime automatically show stock ageing?

The availability of automatic ageing analysis depends on your TallyPrime version, configuration, and any installed extensions. Do not assume that every installation includes a dedicated stock ageing report.

4. What are stock ageing buckets?

Stock ageing buckets are time ranges used to classify inventory, such as 0–30 days, 31–60 days, 61–90 days, and more than 90 days.

5. What is the difference between stock ageing and stock valuation?

Stock ageing measures how long inventory has been held. Stock valuation determines the monetary value of inventory under the applicable accounting method. The two analyses are related but serve different purposes.

6. Can stock ageing help reduce inventory costs?

Yes. It can help businesses identify older inventory, reduce unnecessary purchases, plan promotions, and investigate products that may become obsolete or expire.

7. Can I track expiry dates in TallyPrime?

Expiry tracking depends on the product type and the relevant batch and expiry features supported and configured in your TallyPrime installation. Check the available features and maintain appropriate batch details for expiry-sensitive products.

8. Who should learn stock ageing in TallyPrime?

Accounting students, inventory executives, storekeepers, accountants, business owners, and inventory managers can benefit from understanding stock ageing and inventory movement reports.

CONCLUSION

Track Inventory Age and Make Better Stock Decisions

Tracking stock ageing in TallyPrime helps businesses understand their inventory position, identify products that have remained in stock for extended periods, and make better purchasing and sales decisions.

Start by maintaining accurate inventory records, reviewing stock summaries and movement reports, and using historical receipt information to calculate stock age where necessary. Combine ageing analysis with sales trends, expiry information, and stock valuation to build a more complete picture of inventory performance.

For accounting students and aspiring professionals, learning stock management in TallyPrime is a useful step towards developing practical accounting and inventory control skills.
