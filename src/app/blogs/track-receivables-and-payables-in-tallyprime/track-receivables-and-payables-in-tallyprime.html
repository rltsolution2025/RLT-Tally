How to Track Receivables and Payables in TallyPrime: Step-by-Step Guide

Learn how to track customer receivables and supplier payables, configure bill-wise details, check outstanding bills, monitor due dates, and manage payments in TallyPrime.

Practical Accounting Guide Beginner Friendly

01 · Getting started

Introduction

Tracking receivables and payables is an essential part of accounting and financial management. Businesses need to know how much money customers owe them and how much they must pay suppliers.

Without proper tracking, businesses may face delayed payments, cash flow problems, missed due dates, and difficulties managing working capital.

TallyPrime helps businesses monitor outstanding customer and supplier balances, review bill-wise details, check payment due dates, and maintain accurate accounting records.

Whether you are an accounting student, business owner, accountant, or aspiring accounts executive, learning how to track receivables and payables in TallyPrime can help you manage day-to-day accounting activities more effectively.

In this guide, you will learn what receivables and payables mean, how to configure bill-wise tracking, how to view outstanding reports, and how to use practical examples to understand the process.

02 · Accounting fundamentals

What Are Receivables and Payables in Accounting?

Receivables and payables represent two important parts of a business's credit transactions.

MONEY TO RECEIVE

What Are Accounts Receivable?

Accounts receivable refers to the money customers owe a business for goods or services sold on credit.

EXAMPLE ₹25,000

ABC Enterprises sells goods worth ₹25,000 to a customer on credit. The customer agrees to pay within 30 days.

Until the customer pays, ₹25,000 remains an outstanding receivable.

MONEY TO PAY

What Are Accounts Payable?

Accounts payable refers to the money a business owes suppliers for goods or services purchased on credit.

EXAMPLE ₹15,000

ABC Enterprises purchases office equipment worth ₹15,000 from a supplier on credit. Payment is due within 15 days.

Until the business pays the supplier, ₹15,000 remains an outstanding payable.

03 · Understand the difference

Difference Between Receivables and Payables

Receivables and payables are both related to credit transactions, but they represent different financial responsibilities.

Particulars Accounts Receivable Accounts Payable
Meaning Money customers owe the business Money the business owes suppliers
Nature Asset Liability
Common transaction Credit sales Credit purchases
Amount is collected from Customers Not applicable
Amount is paid to Not applicable Suppliers
Main objective Collect payments on time Pay suppliers on time
TallyPrime report Receivables or outstanding receivables Payables or outstanding payables
Understanding this difference helps accounting students classify transactions correctly and interpret outstanding reports in TallyPrime.

05 · Initial configuration

How to Enable Bill-Wise Tracking in TallyPrime

Bill-wise tracking helps businesses connect payments and receipts to individual invoices rather than relying only on the total ledger balance.

This is useful when customers make partial payments, suppliers issue multiple invoices, or bills have different due dates.

01
Step 1

Open Company Features

Open your company in TallyPrime and access the accounting features or company configuration settings.

02
Step 2

Enable Bill-Wise Details

Locate the option for maintaining bill-wise details and enable it where required.

The exact menu names and navigation may vary depending on your TallyPrime release and company configuration.
03
Step 3

Configure Customer Ledgers

For customer accounts that require invoice-wise tracking:

  1. Open the relevant customer ledger.
  2. Select the appropriate group, generally Sundry Debtors.
  3. Verify the bill-wise tracking configuration.
  4. Enter the applicable credit period, if relevant.
  5. Save the ledger.

You can also learn more about creating ledgers in TallyPrime .

04
Step 4

Configure Supplier Ledgers

For supplier accounts:

  1. Open the supplier ledger.
  2. Select the appropriate group, generally Sundry Creditors.
  3. Verify the bill-wise tracking configuration.
  4. Enter the applicable credit period, if relevant.
  5. Save the ledger.
05
Step 5

Record Transactions with Bill References

When entering credit sales or credit purchases, use the appropriate bill reference details where prompted.

This helps connect subsequent receipts and payments to the correct outstanding bills.

Important: If transactions were entered without the necessary bill-wise details, you may need to review and correct the relevant records before outstanding reports can provide complete invoice-level information.

06 · Customer payments

How to Track Receivables in TallyPrime

Follow these steps to check outstanding amounts customers owe your business.

01
Step 1

Record Credit Sales

Open the Sales voucher and record the transaction using the appropriate customer ledger.

Example

ABC Enterprises sells goods worth ₹30,000 to Customer A on credit.

Enter the invoice details and assign the appropriate bill reference when prompted.

For more information, read TallyPrime Voucher Types Explained .

02
Step 2

Open the Receivables Report

Navigate to the relevant outstanding reports in TallyPrime.

Depending on the version and menu configuration, you can generally find customer outstanding information under the receivables or outstanding bills reports.

Review the report to identify the customer's pending balance.

03
Step 3

Review Individual Customer Balances

Select the relevant customer to inspect the outstanding transactions.

Check the following details:

  • Invoice number.
  • Invoice date.
  • Original invoice amount.
  • Amount received.
  • Remaining outstanding amount.
  • Due date, where configured.

These details help you determine which invoices require follow-up.

04
Step 4

Check Overdue Invoices

Review the due dates and identify invoices that remain unpaid beyond the agreed payment period.

Example

If a customer has an outstanding invoice of ₹30,000 that was due last week, the accounts team can contact the customer and request payment.

05
Step 5

Record Customer Receipts

When the customer makes a payment, record it using the appropriate Receipt voucher.

Allocate the receipt against the correct outstanding bill when bill-wise allocation is available.

This helps reduce the corresponding receivable balance and keeps the outstanding report updated.

07 · Practical example

Practical Example: Tracking Receivables in TallyPrime

Suppose ABC Enterprises makes the following credit sales to Customer A.

Invoice Invoice Amount Amount Received Balance Outstanding
INV-101 ₹20,000 ₹10,000 ₹10,000
INV-102 ₹15,000 ₹0 ₹15,000
INV-103 ₹10,000 ₹5,000 ₹5,000
Total ₹45,000 ₹15,000 ₹30,000
TOTAL OUTSTANDING RECEIVABLE ₹30,000

Amount still due from Customer A.

The accounts team can review each bill, check the applicable due dates, and follow up with the customer for payment.

This example demonstrates why bill-wise tracking is useful: the total outstanding balance alone does not show which invoices are overdue or which invoices have been partially paid.

08 · Supplier payments

How to Track Payables in TallyPrime

Payables tracking helps businesses monitor the amounts owed to suppliers and plan payments according to their due dates.

01
Step 1

Record Credit Purchases

Open the Purchase voucher and record the purchase using the appropriate supplier ledger.

Example

ABC Enterprises purchases materials worth ₹40,000 from Supplier A on credit.

Enter the invoice number, date, amount, and relevant bill reference details.

02
Step 2

Open the Payables or Outstanding Bills Report

Navigate to the relevant outstanding bills or payable reports in TallyPrime.

Review the report to identify pending supplier balances.

03
Step 3

Review Supplier-Wise Outstanding Amounts

Select the supplier to inspect individual bills.

Check:

  • Supplier invoice number.
  • Purchase invoice date.
  • Original invoice amount.
  • Payments already made.
  • Outstanding amount.
  • Due date, where configured.

This helps the accounts team understand how much is payable to each supplier.

04
Step 4

Identify Upcoming Payments

Review payment due dates and prioritise bills according to their contractual terms, available cash, and business requirements.

Example

If a supplier invoice of ₹40,000 is due in three days, the business can plan the payment before the deadline.

05
Step 5

Record Supplier Payments

When a payment is made, record it using the appropriate Payment voucher.

Allocate the payment against the correct supplier bill when bill-wise allocation is available.

The remaining payable balance should then reflect the outstanding amount after the payment is recorded and allocated.

SUP-203 ₹12,000 ₹5,000 ₹7,000
Total ₹55,000 ₹15,000 ₹40,000

The accounts team can use this information to schedule payments, prioritise bills approaching their due dates, and avoid missing agreed payment deadlines.

10 · Outstanding reports

How to View Outstanding Bills and Ageing Reports in TallyPrime

Outstanding bills and ageing reports help businesses understand how long invoices have remained unpaid.

01

What Is an Outstanding Bills Report?

An outstanding bills report displays unpaid or partially paid transactions associated with customers or suppliers.

It helps businesses identify the remaining balance against each bill.

02

What Is an Ageing Report?

An ageing report groups outstanding amounts into time periods based on the configured ageing criteria.

It helps users understand which outstanding bills need attention first.

Illustrative Ageing Report

Ageing Period Outstanding Amount
0–30 days ₹20,000
31–60 days ₹12,000
61–90 days ₹8,000
More than 90 days ₹5,000
Total ₹45,000

This is an illustrative ageing report. The actual date ranges and calculations depend on the report configuration and ageing basis selected.

How to Use Ageing Information

  • Follow up on older overdue receivables.
  • Identify customers who repeatedly delay payments.
  • Review supplier invoices that need urgent settlement.
  • Estimate near-term cash inflows and outflows.
  • Discuss overdue balances with the sales and accounts teams.
Always confirm the report's ageing basis and date range before using it for business decisions.

11 · Accounting practices

Best Practices for Managing Receivables and Payables

01

Record Transactions Promptly

Enter credit sales, purchases, receipts, and payments as soon as practical to maintain current outstanding balances.

02

Use Consistent Bill References

Use appropriate bill references to match payments and receipts with the correct invoices.

03

Set Appropriate Credit Periods

Maintain accurate payment terms in customer and supplier records where supported.

04

Review Outstanding Reports Regularly

Check receivables and payables at suitable intervals, such as daily or weekly, depending on transaction volume.

05

Follow Up on Overdue Receivables

Contact customers with overdue balances and document payment commitments.

06

Plan Supplier Payments

Review upcoming supplier due dates and coordinate payment schedules with available funds.

07

Reconcile Records

Compare accounting records with bank statements, remittance information, and supplier or customer statements.

08

Investigate Unallocated Transactions

12 · Troubleshooting

Common Problems When Tracking Receivables and Payables

01

Outstanding Bills Are Not Displayed Correctly

Possible reason: Bill-wise details may not have been enabled or recorded correctly.

Solution: Review the relevant accounting configuration and transaction allocations.

02

A Customer's Balance Is Incorrect

Possible reason: A receipt may have been entered against the wrong bill or not allocated correctly.

Solution: Review the customer's ledger, receipt vouchers, and bill references.

03

Supplier Payments Are Not Reducing the Correct Invoice

Possible reason: The payment may not have been allocated to the intended supplier bill.

Solution: Review the Payment voucher and its bill-wise allocation.

04

Due Dates Are Missing

Possible reason: Credit periods or bill references may not have been configured or entered correctly.

Solution: Verify the ledger settings, invoice dates, credit terms, and outstanding report configuration.

05

Ageing Reports Do Not Match Expectations

Possible reason: The selected ageing basis, report date, or date ranges may differ from the expected calculation.

Solution: Check the report period and ageing configuration before interpreting the results.

13 · Frequently asked questions

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I check receivables in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant receivables or outstanding bills report and review the customer-wise outstanding balances. Select a customer to inspect invoice-level details where available.

2. How do I check payables in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant payables or outstanding bills report and review the supplier-wise balances. Examine individual supplier bills to check amounts paid and still due.

3. What is bill-wise tracking in TallyPrime?

Bill-wise tracking connects accounting transactions, such as credit sales and purchases, to individual bills. It helps businesses monitor partial payments, outstanding balances, and due dates.

4. What is the difference between receivables and payables?

Receivables represent money customers owe the business and are generally classified as assets. Payables represent money the business owes suppliers and are generally classified as liabilities.

5. Can TallyPrime track overdue customer payments?

Yes. With accurate bill-wise details and due-date information, the relevant outstanding and ageing reports can help identify overdue customer payments.

6. Can TallyPrime track partial payments?

Yes. When payments and receipts are correctly allocated against outstanding bills, the remaining balance can be tracked for partially settled invoices.

7. How often should businesses review receivables and payables?

The review frequency depends on the business's transaction volume and cash flow requirements. Many businesses review important outstanding balances daily or weekly and conduct a more detailed reconciliation periodically.

8. Why are receivables and payables important for accountants?

They help accountants monitor collections, plan supplier payments, maintain accurate ledger balances, identify overdue invoices, and support working capital management.

Tracking receivables and payables in TallyPrime helps businesses maintain better control over customer collections and supplier payments.
