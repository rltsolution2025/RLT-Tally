TallyPrime Practical Test Questions for Accountant Jobs: 25 Questions With Answers

Prepare for accounting job assessments with practical TallyPrime questions, sample transactions, expected entries, inventory tasks, GST basics, and report checks.

▣ October 12, 2026 ◷ 15 min read ✓ Interview preparation

Preparing for an accountant, junior accountant, or accounts assistant job? Knowing TallyPrime is a valuable skill, but employers often want to know whether you can use it to complete everyday accounting tasks accurately.

A TallyPrime practical test may assess your ability to create ledgers, record payment and receipt vouchers, enter purchase and sales transactions, manage inventory, handle GST-related entries, reconcile bank transactions, and generate accounting reports.

In this guide, you will find 25 TallyPrime practical test questions for accountant jobs, sample transactions, expected accounting entries, report-generation tasks, and tips for preparing for a practical assessment.

What Is a TallyPrime Practical Test for Accountant Jobs?

A TallyPrime practical test is an assessment used to evaluate how effectively a candidate can perform accounting activities using TallyPrime. Instead of asking only theoretical questions, an employer may give you a set of business transactions and ask you to enter them into TallyPrime, check the balances, and generate reports.

For example, you may be asked to:

  • Create a company and configure its accounting details.
  • Create customer, supplier, income, and expense ledgers.
  • Record cash and bank transactions.
  • Enter purchase and sales vouchers.
  • Record inventory transactions.
  • Enter GST-related transactions using the applicable tax settings.
  • Generate a Trial Balance, Profit and Loss Account, or Balance Sheet.
  • Perform bank reconciliation.
  • Identify and correct accounting errors.
Remember: The exact test depends on the employer, the job responsibilities, and the level of experience required.

Enter the opening balances in the appropriate ledgers.

Expected result: The opening balances should be reflected correctly in the accounting records. Ensure the complete opening balances are entered consistently so that the books remain balanced.

Account Debit Credit
Office Rent ₹8,000 —
Cash Account — ₹8,000

Expected result: The expense should appear in the appropriate ledger and financial reports.

Record a cash sales transaction

The business sells goods for ₹15,000 in cash, excluding any separately applicable tax.

Account Debit Credit
Cash Account ₹15,000 —
Sales Account — ₹15,000

Expected result: Record the sale using the appropriate sales voucher. If inventory is enabled, select the relevant stock items and quantities where required.

Record a credit purchase

The business purchases goods worth ₹20,000 on credit from XYZ Suppliers, excluding any separately applicable tax.

Account Debit Credit
Purchase Account ₹20,000 —
XYZ Suppliers — ₹20,000

Expected result: The supplier balance should increase by ₹20,000. If inventory is enabled, enter the relevant items and quantities.

The business pays ₹10,000 to XYZ Suppliers through its bank account.

Account Debit Credit
XYZ Suppliers ₹10,000 —
HDFC Bank — ₹10,000

Expected result: The supplier's outstanding balance should reduce by ₹10,000, assuming there are no other transactions.

QUESTION 11

Account Debit Credit

Account Debit Credit
HDFC Bank ₹10,000 —
Cash Account — ₹10,000

The business records ₹2,000 as outstanding office rent at the end of the accounting period.

Account Debit Credit
Office Rent ₹2,000 —
Outstanding Rent — ₹2,000

Expected result: Record the adjustment using a journal voucher and ensure the outstanding expense is classified appropriately.

These entries are simplified examples. Actual voucher selection and tax treatment depend on the transaction and the business's accounting setup.

Section 3: GST and Inventory Practical Questions

QUESTION 14

Create GST-related ledgers

Create the appropriate GST tax ledgers required for the business, based on its registration and transaction requirements.

What the interviewer checks: Whether you understand the configuration of GST-related ledgers and the need to apply the correct tax treatment.

Expected result: The required ledgers and tax settings should be configured correctly for the business.

QUESTION 15

Record a local GST sales transaction

Assume the business sells goods to a customer in the same state and GST applies.

Enter the sales transaction with the correct customer, stock item, taxable value, and applicable GST treatment.

What the interviewer checks: Your ability to select the correct party, stock item, sales ledger, and tax configuration.

Expected result: The sales voucher should calculate tax according to the configured transaction details and applicable rules.

QUESTION 16

Record an interstate sales transaction

Assume the business sells goods to a customer in another state.

Enter the transaction using the appropriate customer and interstate tax treatment, where applicable.

What the interviewer checks: Whether you can distinguish the relevant local and interstate tax treatment and configure the voucher correctly.

Expected result: The tax calculation should follow the applicable rules for the transaction. Do not assume that every interstate sale automatically has the same tax treatment.

Purchase value before tax10 × ₹800 = ₹8,000

Sales value before tax3 × ₹1,200 = ₹3,600

Expected result: Enter the sales voucher with the correct stock item, quantity, rate, customer, and applicable tax details.

QUESTIONS 20–25

Section 4: Accounting Reports and Reconciliation

QUESTION 20

Generate a Trial Balance

Open the Trial Balance for the required accounting period. Check the debit and credit totals and investigate any unexplained difference.

What the interviewer checks: Your ability to access accounting reports and review ledger balances.

QUESTION 21

Generate a Profit and Loss Account

Open the Profit and Loss Account for the required period. Review the income and expense balances and understand how the business's profit or loss is calculated.

Expected result: The report should reflect the income and expenses recorded in the accounting data for the selected period.

QUESTION 22

Generate a Balance Sheet

Open the Balance Sheet as of the required date. Review the assets, liabilities, and capital presented by the report.

Expected result: You should be able to identify the major financial-position categories and investigate unusual balances.

QUESTION 23

Perform bank reconciliation

Compare the bank transactions recorded in TallyPrime with the bank statement. Identify unmatched transactions, pending payments, missing bank charges, or other differences.

Expected result: Reconcile verified matching transactions and investigate remaining differences rather than adjusting balances without evidence.

QUESTION 24

Check outstanding customer balances

Generate the relevant receivables or outstanding report and identify the amount due from Kumar Enterprises.

What the interviewer checks: Your ability to review customer balances and identify pending collections.

Expected result: The outstanding amount should reflect the recorded sales and receipts, along with any other transactions for that customer.

QUESTION 25

Find and correct an accounting error

Suppose an office rent payment of ₹8,000 was accidentally recorded as ₹800.

Identify the incorrect voucher, verify the source document, and correct the transaction using the appropriate alteration or correction process.

Expected result: The ledger should reflect the verified amount of ₹8,000, and the updated financial reports should show the corrected figure.

PRACTICE EXERCISE

Sample TallyPrime Practical Test for Accountant Jobs

Use the following exercise to test your skills before attending an interview.

PRACTICE SCENARIO

ABC Traders

ABC Traders starts business with ₹1,00,000 capital and completes the following transactions.

Date Transaction Amount
1 April Capital introduced in cash ₹1,00,000
2 April Office rent paid in cash ₹8,000
3 April Goods purchased on credit ₹20,000
5 April Cash sales ₹15,000
7 April Payment made to supplier through bank ₹10,000
10 April Customer pays into bank ₹5,000

Your Tasks

  1. Create the company.
  2. Create the necessary ledgers.
  3. Enter all six transactions.
  4. Check the cash and bank balances.
  5. Review the supplier and customer balances.
  6. Generate the Trial Balance.
  7. Open the Profit and Loss Account.
  8. Open the Balance Sheet.
  9. Check whether the debit and credit totals agree.
  10. Explain any differences you find.

Answer Guide

Assuming no opening balances or additional transactions, and treating the purchases and sales as simplified accounting entries without separately calculated taxes:

Item Expected Balance
Closing cash balance ₹1,07,000
Closing bank balance ₹15,000
Supplier outstanding balance ₹10,000
Customer receipt recorded ₹5,000

The cash balance above is calculated from ₹1,00,000 capital less ₹8,000 rent plus ₹15,000 cash sales. The bank balance reflects ₹10,000 paid to the supplier and ₹5,000 received from the customer.

Important: The sample exercise does not include the cost of goods sold, inventory valuation, or GST. If you use inventory and statutory tax settings in TallyPrime, the reports may contain additional entries and balances. Use a consistent accounting setup when checking your answers.

How to Prepare for a TallyPrime Practical Test

If you want to improve your performance in accountant job assessments, practise the following activities regularly.

1

Learn the Common Voucher Types

Understand when to use Payment, Receipt, Contra, Journal, Sales, and Purchase vouchers. Practise selecting the correct voucher for different business transactions.

2

Practise Ledger Creation

Learn how to create ledgers under the correct account groups. Incorrect classification can affect the way accounting reports present balances.

3

Understand Debit and Credit Rules

Learn the basic accounting principles behind assets, liabilities, capital, income, and expenses. This helps you select the correct accounts when recording transactions.

4

Practise Inventory Management

Create stock groups, stock items, and units of measurement. Practise entering purchase and sales transactions with quantities and rates.

5

Understand GST Configuration

Practise setting up relevant tax details and entering sample transactions. Use the correct tax treatment for the business and transaction rather than memorising a single tax entry for every situation.

6

Learn to Generate Accounting Reports

Practise generating and interpreting the Trial Balance, Profit and Loss Account, Balance Sheet, Stock Summary, and outstanding reports.

7

Practise Bank Reconciliation

Compare accounting records with a sample bank statement and identify timing differences, missing transactions, and incorrect entries.

8

Verify Your Entries

After recording transactions, check ledger balances and reports. Accuracy is more important than simply entering vouchers quickly.

CHECK BEFORE YOU SUBMIT

Common Mistakes Candidates Should Avoid

When completing a TallyPrime practical test, avoid these common errors:

  • Selecting the wrong voucher type.
  • Creating ledgers under incorrect groups.
  • Entering incorrect debit or credit amounts.
  • Using the wrong party ledger.
  • Entering incorrect stock quantities or rates.
  • Applying incorrect GST settings.
  • Ignoring outstanding customer and supplier balances.
  • Failing to check the reporting period.
  • Assuming that a balanced Trial Balance proves every transaction is correct.
  • Saving transactions without verifying the supporting details.

A good accountant should be able to enter transactions accurately, understand their accounting impact, and explain the resulting balances.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What practical questions are asked in TallyPrime interviews?

Common questions cover company creation, ledger creation, payment and receipt vouchers, purchase and sales entries, GST configuration, inventory management, bank reconciliation, and financial reports.

2. Is TallyPrime practical knowledge important for accountant jobs?

Yes. Practical knowledge helps candidates demonstrate that they can record transactions, maintain ledgers, review balances, and generate reports. The exact skills required depend on the job description.

3. What should a fresher learn before attending a TallyPrime practical test?

A fresher should understand basic accounting principles, ledger groups, common voucher types, purchase and sales entries, inventory basics, and essential financial reports.

4. Can I prepare for an accountant job using practical questions?

Yes. Practising sample transactions and accounting reports helps you develop familiarity with everyday tasks. You should also practise explaining why you selected a particular voucher or ledger.

5. Which TallyPrime reports should an accountant know?

Accountants should understand the Trial Balance, Profit and Loss Account, Balance Sheet, Day Book, Ledger Vouchers, Stock Summary, receivables and payables reports, and bank reconciliation reports relevant to their role.

6. Do accountant practical tests include GST?

Some employers include GST-related questions, particularly for roles involving purchase invoices, sales invoices, and tax records. Requirements vary by organisation and job level.

7. How can I improve my speed in TallyPrime?

Practise creating masters, entering transactions, using relevant keyboard shortcuts, and reviewing reports. Focus on accuracy first and improve speed through repeated exercises.

8. Are these questions suitable for freshers?

Yes. The questions progress from basic company and ledger creation to vouchers, inventory, GST-related tasks, reconciliation, and financial reports. Candidates can use them for beginner-level preparation and further practice.
