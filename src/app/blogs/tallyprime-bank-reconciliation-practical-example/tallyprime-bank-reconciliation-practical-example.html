Introduction

Bank reconciliation is an essential accounting task that helps businesses verify whether their bank transactions match the records maintained in their accounting software. Even when a business records every receipt and payment correctly, differences can occur because of bank charges, cheque clearance delays, interest credits, or transactions that have not yet been entered into the books.

TallyPrime provides a Bank Reconciliation feature that helps accountants compare transactions recorded in the company's bank ledger with entries appearing in the bank statement.

Learning how to perform bank reconciliation in TallyPrime is useful for accounting students, business owners, accountants, and anyone preparing for a job that involves practical accounting.

In this guide, you will learn what bank reconciliation is, how to reconcile bank transactions in TallyPrime, and how to solve a practical example step by step.

WHAT YOU WILL LEARN

How to review bank ledger entries, enter the actual bank date, investigate unreconciled transactions, record missing bank charges, and explain the remaining difference using a Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS).

What Is Bank Reconciliation?

Bank reconciliation is the process of comparing the bank balance recorded in a company's books with the balance shown in its bank statement and identifying the reasons for any differences.

The comparison is generally performed using the company's bank ledger and the statement provided by the bank.

For example, a business may record a cheque payment on 10 October, but the cheque may be cleared by the bank only on 13 October. Until the cheque is cleared, the balance shown in the company's books may differ from the bank statement.

Bank reconciliation helps accountants identify these timing differences and correct missing or inaccurate accounting entries.

Why Is Bank Reconciliation Important?

Bank reconciliation helps businesses:

  • Identify missing bank transactions.
  • Detect duplicate or incorrect accounting entries.
  • Verify cheque payments and deposits.
  • Record bank charges and interest correctly.
  • Identify unauthorised or unexpected transactions.
  • Maintain accurate cash and bank balances.
  • Prepare reliable financial reports.

Regular reconciliation also helps businesses detect discrepancies before they become more difficult to investigate.

What Is Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime?

Bank reconciliation in TallyPrime is the process of matching entries in the company's bank ledger with the corresponding transactions in the bank statement.

TallyPrime allows users to review bank transactions and enter the relevant bank dates for transactions that have been cleared or credited by the bank.

The Bank Reconciliation feature can help identify:

  • Transactions recorded in the books but not yet reflected in the bank statement.
  • Deposits that have not yet been credited.
  • Cheques that have not yet been cleared.
  • Differences caused by bank charges or interest.
  • Transactions that require investigation or additional accounting entries.
Important: Bank reconciliation does not automatically mean that every difference should be corrected through a journal entry. First identify whether the difference is a normal timing issue or an error that requires an accounting adjustment.

What Do You Need Before Starting Bank Reconciliation?

Before beginning the process in TallyPrime, keep the following information ready:

  1. Your company data in TallyPrime.
  2. The relevant bank ledger.
  3. The bank statement for the reconciliation period.
  4. The accounting entries for receipts, payments, deposits, and withdrawals.
  5. Details of bank charges, interest, or other bank-initiated transactions.
  6. The opening bank balance and previous reconciliation details, if applicable.

Make sure the bank statement covers the period you want to reconcile.

Correct bank ledger

Choose the ledger that represents the bank account covered by the statement. If the business has multiple accounts, reconcile them separately.

Complete bank statement

Use the statement for the same reconciliation period and retain the transaction references needed to verify receipts and payments.

How to Perform Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime: Step-by-Step Guide

Follow these steps to reconcile bank transactions in TallyPrime. Menu names may vary slightly depending on the release and configuration.

01

Open Your Company in TallyPrime

Launch TallyPrime and select the company whose bank transactions you want to reconcile. Verify that you have selected the correct company and financial period.

02

Open the Bank Ledger

Navigate to the relevant bank account ledger or the Bank Reconciliation report. In many TallyPrime releases, you can access the report through the banking or reconciliation options, or by opening the bank ledger and selecting the reconciliation feature. Select the bank account you want to reconcile, such as HDFC Bank, SBI, or another business bank account.

03

Open the Bank Reconciliation Report

Open the Bank Reconciliation screen for the selected bank ledger. Depending on your configuration, the report may display the accounting date, voucher number, transaction details, debit or credit amount, bank date, and reconciliation status. Review the report before making changes.

04

Compare Transactions with the Bank Statement

Take the bank statement and compare it with the entries shown in TallyPrime. Check the transaction date, amount, payment or receipt reference, cheque number, UTR or other available reference, and whether the transaction appears in the statement.

For example, if a cheque payment of ₹5,000 appears in TallyPrime but has not yet been cleared by the bank, it should not be treated as a completed bank transaction for reconciliation purposes.

05

Enter the Bank Date

For a transaction that appears in both the books and the bank statement, enter the corresponding bank date in the reconciliation screen. The bank date is the date on which the transaction was cleared, credited, or otherwise reflected by the bank.

Example: accounting entry date 10 October 2026, bank clearance date 13 October 2026, payment amount ₹5,000. If the transaction appears in the statement on 13 October, enter that date in the relevant bank reconciliation field.

Use the actual date shown in the bank statement rather than assuming the bank processed the transaction on the accounting entry date.

06

Identify Unreconciled Transactions

Review transactions that still do not match. Common reasons include cheques issued but not presented, cheques deposited but not credited, bank charges or interest not entered, incorrect amounts or dates, duplicate entries, and missing receipts or payments.

Investigate each difference before deciding whether to enter a bank date or record an accounting adjustment.

07

Record Missing Bank Transactions

If the bank statement contains a transaction that is not recorded in TallyPrime, create the appropriate accounting voucher. For example, if the bank deducts ₹250 as bank charges, enter the expense using the appropriate bank account and bank charges ledger.

08

Verify the Reconciled Balance

Once you have matched the relevant transactions and recorded necessary missing entries, review the reconciliation report again. Check that cleared transactions have correct bank dates, outstanding items remain identifiable, bank charges and interest are recorded correctly, no transactions have been reconciled twice, and any remaining difference is explained.

Save changes using the options available in your TallyPrime version.

The bank date should reflect the actual date shown in the bank statement. Do not change dates simply to make the balances appear equal.

For more detail about the underlying entries, see How to Record Bank Transactions in TallyPrime.

TallyPrime Bank Reconciliation: Practical Example

Let us understand bank reconciliation using a simple business example. ABC Traders maintains a bank account and records the following transactions in TallyPrime during October 2026.

STEP 1 · TRANSACTIONS IN TALLYPRIME

Transactions recorded in the books

Date Transaction Amount
1 October Opening bank balance ₹50,000
5 October Customer payment received ₹20,000
8 October Supplier payment by cheque ₹10,000
10 October Office expenses paid ₹5,000
12 October Customer cheque deposited ₹8,000

The closing balance according to the books is:

₹50,000 + ₹20,000 − ₹10,000 − ₹5,000 + ₹8,000₹63,000
STEP 2 · BANK STATEMENT

Transactions appearing in the bank statement

Suppose the bank statement shows the following transactions by the end of the period.

Transaction Amount
Opening bank balance ₹50,000
Customer payment credited ₹20,000
Supplier cheque cleared −₹10,000
Office expenses paid −₹5,000
Bank charges −₹250
Closing bank statement balance ₹54,750

The customer cheque of ₹8,000 has not yet been credited by the bank, and bank charges of ₹250 have not yet been recorded in TallyPrime.

STEP 3 · IDENTIFY THE DIFFERENCES

Two differences need investigation

Difference 1: Customer cheque deposited but not credited

The business has recorded the ₹8,000 deposit in TallyPrime, but it does not yet appear in the bank statement. This is a timing difference. Keep the deposit identifiable as awaiting credit until the bank processes it.

Difference 2: Bank charges of ₹250

The bank has deducted ₹250, but the business has not recorded the expense in TallyPrime. This is a missing accounting entry that should be recorded.

STEP 4 · RECORD BANK CHARGES

Enter the missing bank charge

Particulars Debit Credit
Bank Charges A/c ₹250 —
Bank A/c — ₹250
Total ₹250 ₹250

The adjusted bank balance according to the books becomes:

₹63,000 − ₹250₹62,750
STEP 5 · BANK RECONCILIATION STATEMENT

Compare the adjusted book balance with the statement

Particulars Amount
Adjusted balance according to books ₹62,750
Less: Deposit not yet credited by the bank ₹8,000
Balance according to bank statement ₹54,750
RECONCILIATION RESULT

The balances agree after considering the outstanding deposit. The missing bank charge has been recorded in the books, while the ₹8,000 deposit remains a timing difference until the bank credits it.

This example demonstrates the basic principle of bank reconciliation: record missing transactions in the books and identify valid timing differences rather than treating every difference as an error.

Common Bank Reconciliation Errors in TallyPrime

1. Incorrect Bank Date

Entering the accounting date instead of the actual bank transaction date can cause reconciliation errors.

Solution: Check the transaction date in the bank statement and enter the correct bank date.

2. Missing Bank Charges

Banks may deduct service charges, cheque return charges, or other fees that are not immediately recorded in the books.

Solution: Record the appropriate expense and bank entry, then reconcile the transaction.

3. Duplicate Transactions

A transaction may have been entered twice in the accounting records.

Solution: Compare the voucher number, amount, date, and transaction reference with the bank statement before correcting the duplicate.

4. Cheques Not Yet Cleared

A cheque payment may appear in TallyPrime but not in the bank statement because the recipient has not presented it or the bank has not cleared it.

Solution: Keep the cheque identifiable as outstanding and reconcile it when it appears in the bank statement.

5. Deposits Not Yet Credited

A cheque or deposit recorded in the books may take time to appear in the bank statement.

Solution: Check the deposit details and bank processing status. Reconcile the entry when the bank credits it.

6. Incorrect Amounts

A mismatch may occur because of an incorrect voucher amount or a bank transaction recorded with the wrong value.

Solution: Compare the original supporting document and bank statement, then correct the accounting record where necessary.

Best Practices for Bank Reconciliation

Follow these practices to improve accounting accuracy:

  • Reconcile bank accounts regularly, preferably every month or more frequently where needed.
  • Download or obtain the correct bank statement for the period.
  • Use transaction references to match receipts and payments.
  • Record bank charges and interest promptly.
  • Investigate old outstanding cheques and deposits.
  • Avoid changing bank dates simply to eliminate a difference.
  • Maintain supporting documents for adjustments.
  • Review reconciled and unreconciled transactions before closing the period.
  • Keep a record of unresolved discrepancies and follow up with the bank where necessary.

Regular reconciliation helps businesses maintain reliable financial records and detect problems early.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is bank reconciliation in TallyPrime?

Bank reconciliation in TallyPrime is the process of comparing transactions recorded in a company's bank ledger with the transactions shown in the bank statement and identifying the reasons for any differences.

2. How do I open Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant company, navigate to the bank ledger or available banking reports, and select the Bank Reconciliation option. The exact navigation may vary depending on the TallyPrime release.

3. What is a bank date in TallyPrime?

A bank date is the date on which a transaction is reflected or cleared by the bank, as shown in the bank statement. It may differ from the date on which the transaction was recorded in the company's books.

4. Why does my TallyPrime bank balance differ from my bank statement?

Differences may arise from outstanding cheques, deposits awaiting credit, bank charges, interest, missing entries, duplicate transactions, or incorrect amounts and dates.

5. How do I record bank charges in TallyPrime?

Create or select the appropriate bank charges expense ledger and record a payment or accounting voucher that debits bank charges and credits the bank account. Then reconcile the entry against the bank statement.

6. Do I need to reconcile my bank account every month?

Monthly reconciliation is a common practice because it helps businesses identify discrepancies and prepare accurate financial reports. Businesses with frequent transactions may benefit from more frequent reconciliation.

7. What is the difference between bank reconciliation and a bank reconciliation statement?

Bank reconciliation is the process of comparing bank records with accounting records. A bank reconciliation statement (BRS) summarises the differences between the two balances and explains the adjustments or timing differences that cause them.

8. Can beginners learn bank reconciliation using TallyPrime?

Yes. Beginners can practise matching bank transactions, entering bank dates, recording bank charges, identifying outstanding cheques, and preparing reconciliation statements using a sample company.

Conclusion

Bank reconciliation is an important practical accounting skill that helps businesses verify their bank balances and maintain accurate financial records.

Using TallyPrime, accountants can compare bank statement transactions with entries in the bank ledger, enter the correct bank dates, identify unreconciled transactions, and record missing bank charges or interest.

The practical example in this guide demonstrates how to identify outstanding deposits and missing bank charges, adjust the book balance, and explain the remaining difference.

For accounting students and aspiring accountants, practising bank reconciliation in TallyPrime is a valuable step towards developing real-world accounting skills.
