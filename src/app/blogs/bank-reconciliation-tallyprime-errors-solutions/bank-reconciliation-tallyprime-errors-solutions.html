01 / INTRODUCTION

Introduction

Bank reconciliation is an essential accounting process that helps businesses verify whether their accounting records match their bank statements. Even when transactions are recorded regularly, differences can occur because of bank charges, unrecorded payments, cheque clearance delays, incorrect entries, or other timing differences.

TallyPrime provides bank reconciliation features that help accountants compare bank transactions with entries recorded in the company's books, identify discrepancies, and update reconciliation details.

Understanding bank reconciliation in TallyPrime is especially useful for accounting students, aspiring accountants, business owners, and professionals who handle daily financial transactions.

In this guide, you will learn how to perform bank reconciliation in TallyPrime, identify common errors, understand their causes, and follow practical solutions to maintain accurate bank records.

What you will learn

How to review the bank ledger, compare statement transactions, enter bank dates, resolve unmatched entries, and correct common reconciliation differences without forcing an unsupported match.

02 / DEFINITION

What Is Bank Reconciliation?

Bank reconciliation is the process of comparing the bank balance recorded in a company's books with the balance shown in its bank statement and explaining any differences between them.

For example, a business may record a cheque payment on Monday, but the bank may process it only on Wednesday. During this period, the company's accounting records and bank statement may show different balances.

Bank reconciliation helps identify these differences and determine whether they are caused by timing, missing transactions, incorrect entries, or genuine errors.

Reconciliation is not simply making two balances equal. It is identifying and explaining every relevant difference using the supporting accounting and bank records.
03 / BENEFITS

Why Is Bank Reconciliation Important?

Bank reconciliation helps businesses:

Identify Missing Entries

Find bank charges, interest credits, and other transactions that have not been recorded.

Detect Accounting Errors

Identify incorrect amounts, duplicate entries, and wrong transaction details.

Track Pending Payments

Monitor cheques and other payments that have not yet cleared the bank.

Verify Bank Balances

Understand why the accounting balance differs from the bank statement.

Support Audits

Maintain clearer financial records for accountants, auditors, and business owners.

Improve Financial Control

Keep banking records updated for better cash-flow monitoring.

Regular reconciliation also helps accountants identify unusual transactions before they become larger accounting problems.

04 / TALLYPRIME

What Is Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime?

Bank reconciliation in TallyPrime is the process of matching transactions recorded in the company's bank ledger with the corresponding transactions in the bank statement.

TallyPrime provides a Bank Reconciliation feature for supported bank ledgers. Depending on the bank, software version, and configuration, users may also have access to bank statement import and automatic reconciliation features.

During reconciliation, accountants typically review transaction dates, amounts, and other available details to identify matching entries and determine when transactions were cleared by the bank.

Important: Bank reconciliation does not automatically correct every accounting error. Missing transactions, incorrect amounts, and duplicate entries may require separate accounting corrections.

For a separate walkthrough of the full reconciliation process, read How to Perform Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime.

05 / STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE

How to Perform Bank Reconciliation in TallyPrime

Follow these steps to reconcile a bank account in TallyPrime. The exact menu names may vary slightly by release.

01

Open the Bank Ledger

Open TallyPrime, select the relevant company, navigate to the banking or account reports section, and open the Bank Reconciliation report for the required bank ledger and period. If the ledger is unavailable, verify that it has been created and grouped correctly under Bank Accounts.

02

Review the Bank Transactions

Examine transaction date, payment or receipt amount, voucher type, bank reference or cheque number where available, and narration. Confirm that the entries are consistent with supporting accounting documents.

03

Compare Transactions with the Bank Statement

Obtain the bank statement for the same period. Compare payments, receipts, transfers, bank charges, and interest credits. Entries with matching amounts and relevant details can generally be matched during reconciliation.

04

Enter the Bank Date

For transactions recorded in TallyPrime with a different bank-clearing date, enter or verify the applicable bank date. For example, a cheque issued on 10 October may clear on 13 October. Use the date shown in the bank statement.

05

Resolve Unmatched Transactions

Investigate each item that appears in one record but not the other. Determine whether it is a timing issue, missing entry, incorrect amount, duplicate transaction, or unidentified bank transaction. Do not force a match merely to make balances agree.

06

Verify the Reconciled Balance

Review the reconciliation report and compare the reconciled balance with the bank statement. Ensure that remaining differences are understood and supported by relevant records. Retain the statement and supporting documents for future reference and audit requirements.

For more details about voucher entry used to record missing bank transactions, see TallyPrime Voucher Types Explained.

06 / TROUBLESHOOTING

Bank Reconciliation: Common Errors and Solutions

The following are common problems accountants may encounter while reconciling bank accounts in TallyPrime.

1. Bank Balance Does Not Match the Bank Statement

Problem: The closing balance in TallyPrime differs from the closing balance shown in the bank statement.

Possible causes: Outstanding cheques, deposits not yet credited, missing bank charges, incorrect payment or receipt entries, or transactions recorded with incorrect dates.

Solution: Compare opening and closing balances for the same period. Review outstanding transactions and identify bank statement entries not recorded in TallyPrime. Correct genuine accounting errors and reconcile timing differences using appropriate bank dates.

2. Cheque Issued but Not Cleared

Problem: A cheque payment appears in TallyPrime, but the corresponding amount has not yet been deducted from the bank account.

Possible cause: The cheque has been issued but has not yet been presented or cleared by the bank.

Solution: Verify the cheque details and confirm that the payment entry is correct. If it remains outstanding, retain it as an outstanding item until the bank processes it or the transaction otherwise requires adjustment. Do not delete a valid payment simply because the cheque has not cleared.

3. Deposit Recorded but Not Credited

Problem: A receipt or deposit appears in the company's accounting records, but the bank statement does not yet show the corresponding credit.

Possible causes: The deposit is still being processed, the bank has not credited it, it was recorded incorrectly, or it was deposited into another account.

Solution: Verify the deposit slip, payment reference, amount, and bank account. Track deposits in transit until they appear in the statement. Correct an accounting entry only after confirming the actual transaction details.

4. Bank Charges Are Missing in TallyPrime

Problem: The bank statement contains service charges, SMS fees, cheque-book charges, or other bank fees that are absent from the accounting records.

Possible cause: The bank deducted the charges, but the accountant did not record them in TallyPrime.

Solution: Verify the charge against the bank statement and record an appropriate payment or journal entry based on the accounting workflow.

Example: Bank Charges of ₹250

Accounting Entry
Bank Charges A/c (Debit)₹250
Bank A/c (Credit)₹250
Total Debit / Credit₹250 / ₹250

After saving the entry, return to the reconciliation report and match the bank transaction.

5. Interest Credited by the Bank Is Missing

Problem: The bank statement shows interest income, but TallyPrime does not contain the corresponding entry.

Possible cause: The interest was credited automatically by the bank without a separate accounting entry being recorded.

Solution: Confirm the interest amount and date from the bank statement. Record the income using the appropriate accounting voucher.

Example: Interest Received of ₹500

Accounting Entry
Bank A/c (Debit)₹500
Interest Income A/c (Credit)₹500
Total Debit / Credit₹500 / ₹500

Once the entry is recorded, reconcile it with the bank statement.

6. Duplicate Transactions in TallyPrime

Problem: The same payment or receipt appears more than once in the accounting records.

Possible causes: A voucher was entered twice, an imported statement was processed incorrectly, or an accountant created a second entry after failing to locate the original voucher.

Solution: Compare dates, amounts, references, and supporting documents. If a duplicate is confirmed, correct or reverse it using the appropriate accounting procedure and authorisation. Do not remove a legitimate repeated payment merely because the amounts are identical.

7. Incorrect Transaction Amount

Problem: The amount recorded in TallyPrime differs from the amount shown in the bank statement.

Possible causes: A typing error, incorrect cheque details, wrong payment or receipt amount, or bank fees not accounted for separately.

Solution: Compare the original voucher with the bank statement and supporting documents. Correct the accounting entry if the amount was entered incorrectly. If the difference is a separate bank charge, record it appropriately rather than changing the original amount without justification.

8. Incorrect Bank Date

Problem: A transaction appears under the wrong date or remains unreconciled even though the bank has processed it.

Possible cause: The bank-clearing date was entered incorrectly or does not match the bank statement.

Solution: Verify the transaction's actual processing date from the bank statement. Update the bank reconciliation details using the correct date. Remember that the voucher date and bank-clearing date can legitimately be different.

9. Bank Statement Import or Matching Errors

Problem: Imported bank transactions do not match accounting entries, or some transactions remain unmatched.

Possible causes: An unsupported or incorrectly formatted statement, incorrect date or amount formats, duplicate imports, inconsistent descriptions or reference numbers, or missing accounting vouchers.

Solution: Verify the statement format supported by your TallyPrime version and selected bank. Check import settings and transaction details. Investigate unmatched transactions before creating a new voucher to avoid duplication.

10. Opening Balance Difference

Problem: The reconciliation report shows a difference at the beginning of the period.

Possible causes: Incorrect opening bank balance, prior-period transactions not reconciled, errors carried forward, or changes made to previously recorded vouchers.

Solution: Review the opening balance, prior-period bank statements, and previous reconciliation reports. Identify the original cause before adjusting the opening balance. Make any necessary correction only after checking supporting records and documenting the change.

07 / PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

Practical Example: Fixing a Bank Reconciliation Difference

Consider a business that has the following transactions in its bank ledger and bank statement.

Particulars Amount
Balance as per bank statement before listed transactions ₹50,000
Customer payment credited by bank +₹10,000
Supplier payment cleared by bank −₹8,000
Bank charges −₹250
Closing bank statement balance ₹51,750

Suppose TallyPrime records the customer receipt and supplier payment but does not include the bank charges.

BOOK BALANCE BEFORE BANK CHARGES
₹50,000 + ₹10,000 − ₹8,000 = ₹52,000
BANK STATEMENT BALANCE
₹52,000 − ₹250 = ₹51,750
Difference to investigate: ₹250

How to Resolve the Difference

  1. Verify the ₹250 charge in the bank statement.
  2. Record the bank charges in TallyPrime.
  3. Match the bank charge with the corresponding bank transaction.
  4. Review the reconciliation report again.

After recording the missing charge, the accounting bank balance becomes ₹51,750, assuming there are no other outstanding items or errors.

This example demonstrates how identifying one missing transaction can resolve a reconciliation difference.

08 / BEST PRACTICES

How to Prevent Bank Reconciliation Errors in TallyPrime

Resolving discrepancies is important, but preventing them is even better. Follow these practices:

Record Transactions Regularly
Enter payments, receipts, and transfers promptly.
Review Statements Frequently
Reconcile regularly instead of waiting until year-end.
Verify Transaction Details
Check dates, amounts, cheque numbers, and references.
Record Charges and Interest
Review automatic bank transactions and enter them.
Avoid Duplicate Entries
Search accounting records before creating new vouchers.
Maintain Supporting Documents
Keep statements, deposit slips, and confirmations.
Review Unreconciled Items
Investigate old cheques and pending deposits.
Use Import Carefully
Review configuration and matching results before acceptance.
Protect Accounting Data
Use permissions, backups, and internal controls.

A consistent bank reconciliation process helps businesses maintain reliable accounting records and identify discrepancies early.

09 / FAQS

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I perform bank reconciliation in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant company, access the Bank Reconciliation report for the bank ledger, compare the recorded transactions with the bank statement, enter or verify bank dates, and investigate unmatched items. Review the final reconciliation report before closing the period.

2. Why is my bank balance different in TallyPrime?

The difference may be caused by outstanding cheques, deposits in transit, missing bank charges, unrecorded interest, incorrect amounts, duplicate entries, or an opening balance error. Compare both records and identify the underlying cause before making adjustments.

3. How do I record bank charges in TallyPrime?

Record the bank charges using an appropriate payment or accounting voucher. Typically, bank charges are debited to an expense ledger and the bank ledger is credited. Then reconcile the entry against the bank statement.

4. What is an unreconciled transaction?

An unreconciled transaction is an entry that has not yet been matched or confirmed against the corresponding bank statement transaction. It may represent a legitimate timing difference, a missing entry, or an accounting error.

5. Can TallyPrime automatically reconcile bank transactions?

Depending on the TallyPrime release, supported bank, and configuration, bank statement import and automated matching features may be available. Transactions that cannot be matched confidently may still require manual review.

6. How often should a business perform bank reconciliation?

The frequency depends on transaction volume, business requirements, and internal controls. Many businesses reconcile monthly, while businesses with frequent transactions may benefit from weekly or daily checks.

7. Should I delete a transaction if it does not match the bank statement?

No. First determine why the transaction does not match. It may be a valid outstanding cheque, an uncleared deposit, or a timing difference. Correct or reverse an entry only after confirming that it is genuinely wrong or duplicated.

8. Is bank reconciliation important for accounting students?

Yes. Bank reconciliation is a practical accounting skill that helps students understand cash and bank ledgers, payment and receipt vouchers, transaction matching, and error correction. These skills are useful for entry-level accounting and accounts executive roles.

10 / CONCLUSION

Conclusion

Bank reconciliation in TallyPrime helps businesses maintain accurate bank records and identify differences between accounting entries and bank statements.

Common problems include unmatched transactions, uncleared cheques, deposits in transit, missing bank charges, duplicate entries, incorrect amounts, and opening balance differences. By identifying the underlying cause and applying the appropriate correction, accountants can maintain more reliable financial records.

For accounting students and aspiring professionals, practising bank reconciliation in TallyPrime is a valuable way to develop practical accounting skills and prepare for real-world financial tasks.
