INTRODUCTION

How to Set Reorder Levels in TallyPrime

Managing inventory efficiently is important for businesses that sell products, manufacture goods, or maintain stock for daily operations. If a business runs out of essential items, it may lose sales, delay customer orders, or interrupt production. Keeping too much stock, on the other hand, can increase storage costs and tie up working capital.

TallyPrime helps businesses monitor inventory and identify when stock needs replenishment through features such as reorder levels and reorder status reports.

In this guide, you will learn what reorder levels are, why they are important, how to configure them in TallyPrime, and how to use reorder reports to make better inventory decisions. This tutorial also includes practical examples to help accounting students and beginners understand inventory management.

What will you learn?

Understand reorder thresholds, configure stock-item settings, review reorder status, and calculate a reorder point using demand, supplier lead time, and safety stock.

BASICS

What Is a Reorder Level in TallyPrime?

A reorder level is a predefined stock quantity or threshold that indicates when a business should consider ordering more inventory.

When the available stock reaches the specified level, the business can review its inventory and plan a purchase to avoid running out of stock.

For example, suppose a shop sells printer paper and usually needs at least 20 reams available to meet customer demand. The business can set a reorder level of 20 reams and review its stock position when the quantity reaches that threshold.

TallyPrime's reorder-level feature helps businesses monitor stock levels and identify items that may require replenishment.

Important: A reorder level is a planning threshold, not necessarily the exact quantity that must be purchased. The required purchase quantity depends on current stock, pending orders, expected demand, supplier lead time, and the business's stocking policy.
BUSINESS BENEFITS

Why Are Reorder Levels Important in Inventory Management?

Setting reorder levels can help businesses improve stock control and avoid unnecessary inventory problems.

Prevent Stockouts

Identify items running low and plan purchases before stock becomes insufficient.

Reduce Excess Inventory

Use reorder levels alongside demand forecasts to avoid unnecessary purchases.

Improve Purchase Planning

Prioritise products that require replenishment and plan purchase orders.

Maintain Continuity

Reduce the risk of operational delays caused by unavailable materials or products.

Manage Working Capital

Balance product availability against the money tied up in stock.

Support Decisions

Use inventory reports to identify which products need attention.

KEY DIFFERENCE

What Is the Difference Between Reorder Level and Minimum Stock Level?

Reorder level and minimum stock level are related inventory concepts, but they serve different purposes.

Feature Reorder Level Minimum Stock Level
Meaning A threshold that triggers replenishment planning A lower stock limit the business aims to avoid falling below
Main purpose Helps determine when to order Helps maintain a minimum stock buffer
Depends on Demand, supplier lead time, and stock policy Business risk, demand variability, and safety stock
Example Place or review an order when stock reaches 50 units Aim to keep at least 20 units available

The terms may be used differently by different businesses. A company should define its inventory policy clearly so that reorder thresholds and minimum stock requirements are not confused.

BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Prerequisites Before Setting Reorder Levels in TallyPrime

Before configuring reorder levels, check the following:

  • Inventory features are enabled for the company.
  • The relevant stock items have been created.
  • Units of measurement are configured correctly.
  • Opening stock and inventory transactions are recorded accurately.
  • Purchase and sales entries are up to date.
  • The business has estimated its stock requirements and supplier lead times.

Correct stock-item configuration is essential because incorrect units or stock balances can affect reorder calculations and reports.

STEP-BY-STEP TUTORIAL

How to Set Reorder Levels in TallyPrime

Follow these steps to configure reorder levels for stock items. Menu names may vary slightly depending on the TallyPrime release.

01

Open Your Company in TallyPrime

Launch TallyPrime and select the company in which you want to manage inventory. Verify that inventory features are enabled and that your stock items and transactions are available.

02

Create or Select a Stock Item

Open the stock-item section and select an existing item or create a new one. For example, use Printer Paper with Ream as its unit of measurement and Office Supplies as its stock group. Verify the applicable inventory details.

03

Locate the Reorder-Level Settings

Open the relevant stock-item configuration or alteration screen and check whether reorder-level details are available in your TallyPrime version. Use the in-product search or help facility if the option is not immediately visible.

04

Enter the Reorder Level

Suppose the business wants to review replenishment whenever Printer Paper stock reaches 20 reams. Enter the reorder threshold as 20 reams, using the appropriate unit and configuration.

05

Save the Configuration

After entering the required information, save the stock-item configuration. Verify that the reorder level has been recorded correctly and that the unit matches the stock item.

06

Repeat for Other Stock Items

Configure appropriate reorder thresholds for other relevant products. Set item-specific thresholds based on demand, supplier lead time, and your inventory policy.

The exact stock-item settings and navigation may differ by TallyPrime release. Confirm the available reorder-level fields in your installed version before following version-specific screenshots.

PRACTICAL CALCULATION

Practical Example: Reorder Levels in TallyPrime

Let's understand reorder-level planning through a practical example. ABC Stationery sells notebooks and wants to maintain sufficient inventory for its customers.

Particulars Value
Stock item A4 Notebook
Average daily demand 5 units
Supplier lead time 4 days
Safety stock 10 units
Current stock 25 units

Step 1: Calculate the Reorder Point

A common inventory-planning formula is:

REORDER POINT FORMULA Reorder point = Average daily demand × Supplier lead time + Safety stock

Using the example

Reorder point = 5 × 4 + 10

Reorder point = 30 units

The business should therefore plan replenishment when its inventory position reaches approximately 30 units, subject to its stocking policy.

Step 2: Compare the Reorder Point with Current Stock

Current stock is 25 units, while the calculated reorder point is 30 units. Because the stock is below the planned reorder point, the business should review its replenishment requirement.

Step 3: Estimate the Purchase Quantity

Suppose the business wants to restore inventory to 60 units and has no outstanding purchase orders.

PURCHASE QUANTITYEstimated purchase quantity = Target stock − Current stock

60 − 25 = 35 units

This calculation is a simplified example. In practice, consider outstanding orders, committed sales, supplier lead time, minimum order quantities, and expected demand before placing the order.

TROUBLESHOOTING

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Setting Reorder Levels

1. Setting the Same Reorder Level for Every Item

Different products have different demand patterns and supplier lead times. Solution: Use item-specific thresholds wherever appropriate.

2. Ignoring Supplier Lead Time

Products with longer delivery times may need higher reorder points. Solution: Consider replenishment time when defining each threshold.

3. Using Incorrect Stock Units

Mixing boxes, pieces, and cartons can cause planning errors. Solution: Verify the unit of measurement for each stock item.

4. Failing to Update Stock Transactions

Missing or inaccurate purchase, sales, or adjustment entries can make reports misleading. Solution: Keep inventory transactions up to date.

5. Ignoring Outstanding Purchase Orders

Ordering without considering stock already on order may create excess inventory. Solution: Review pending purchase quantities before placing another order.

6. Never Reviewing Reorder Levels

Demand, supplier prices, delivery times, and seasonal requirements can change. Solution: Review reorder settings periodically.

7. Confusing Reorder Level with Purchase Quantity

A reorder level indicates when replenishment should be considered. Solution: Calculate the purchase quantity separately using current stock and expected demand.

INVENTORY MANAGEMENT

Best Practices for Managing Inventory in TallyPrime

To make inventory planning more effective, follow these practices:

  • Set realistic reorder thresholds for important stock items.
  • Use accurate opening stock and inventory transaction records.
  • Review inventory reports regularly.
  • Monitor fast-moving and slow-moving items separately.
  • Consider supplier lead times and seasonal demand.
  • Track outstanding purchase orders.
  • Maintain consistent units of measurement.
  • Review inventory settings when demand patterns change.
  • Use appropriate purchase and stock reports to support decisions.

These practices can help businesses maintain a better balance between stock availability and inventory costs.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a reorder level in TallyPrime?

A reorder level is a predefined inventory threshold used to identify when a stock item may need replenishment. It helps businesses plan purchases before inventory becomes insufficient.

2. How do I set reorder levels in TallyPrime?

Open the relevant stock-item configuration and locate the reorder-level settings supported by your version. Enter the appropriate threshold, save the configuration, and review the available reorder status report.

3. Can TallyPrime help identify stock items that need reordering?

TallyPrime provides inventory reporting features that can help businesses review stock positions and replenishment requirements. The exact information available depends on the version, configuration, and accuracy of recorded transactions.

4. What is the difference between reorder level and reorder quantity?

Reorder level determines when a business should consider placing an order. Reorder quantity is the amount purchased to replenish stock. The two values serve different inventory-management purposes.

5. How do I calculate a reorder point?

A common formula is: Reorder point = Average daily demand × Supplier lead time + Safety stock. For example, if daily demand is 5 units, supplier lead time is 4 days, and safety stock is 10 units, the reorder point is 30 units.

6. Does setting a reorder level automatically place a purchase order?

Do not assume that configuring a reorder threshold automatically places an order. Review the available TallyPrime features and your company's purchasing workflow, then confirm and record the required purchase transaction.

7. Can I set different reorder levels for different stock items?

Businesses should use appropriate thresholds for different products based on demand, replenishment time, and stocking requirements. Configure the available stock-item settings according to your TallyPrime version.

8. Why is inventory management important for accounting students?

Inventory management helps accounting students understand stock valuation, purchase transactions, sales records, inventory reports, and working capital. These skills are useful in accounting, inventory control, and operations-related roles.
